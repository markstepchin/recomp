How I test progress photo apps: the method, every score, and all the data

By Mark Stepchin · Updated [CONFIRM: publish date]

Quick Answer

I test every progress photo app with the same photos, the same steps and the same scorecard, and this page holds all of it. Seven scores of 1 to 5, grouped under five needs, make a grade out of 35, with import as a bonus kept out of the total. Most grades are still partial: 6 of 10 apps have proposed scores, and none has all seven. The rankings in my roundup of the best progress photo apps come from these tables.

Full disclosure: I build Recomp, one of the 10 apps I tested, and I wrote this rubric. Recomp is free with no in-app purchases, and no app pays me [CONFIRM: no ads, affiliate links or partner deals]. I built Recomp around the same needs this rubric scores, so weigh Recomp's grade with that in mind. Import, Recomp's strength, stays out of the total for that reason.

Why I test this way

Star ratings told me little about how these apps handle a real photo library. Four apps average 4.4 to 4.9 stars on 9,819 ratings between them, and each scored 1 or 2 out of 5 on comparison. Rating counts track an app's age more than its quality: Then & Now has 9 ratings and scored 3 on comparison, and Lasmit has 9,355 and scored 2.

Before testing, I read the 15 roundups that rank for this search. Two compared listed features without testing. Eight show no screenshots, several recommend apps last updated in 2017, and most put their own app first. I put mine first too, so every claim in my roundup has a screenshot from my own photos and a row in a table here.

I've taken 450+ weekly progress photos [CONFIRM] since [CONFIRM: year]. Each week I shot front, side and back and logged my weight. More than 10 dedicated apps failed to stick before I built Recomp.

What I score, and why

The scores follow the five needs I built Recomp on. I explain how to judge an app against them in what to look for in a progress photo app [CONFIRM: REC-109 live before linking].

  1. Tracking must be effortless. If a check-in takes more than a minute and a few taps, consistency drops. The loop, from the reminder to a side-by-side with last week, should fit in that minute.
  2. The app needs to stay out of the way. Without precise alignment and instant moves between dates, I couldn't tell whether I was progressing.
  3. It's a power tool. Weekly front, side and back shots add up to 156 photos a year, and an imported archive can start in the hundreds on day one.
  4. It's a specialized tool. A photo app should do photos and weight well, and leave workouts and food to the apps built for them.
  5. Your photos stay private. Stored on your phone, never uploaded, with no account. A backup to your own iCloud or computer still counts as private.
Need Score (1 to 5) What it measures
Effortless tracking Capture and tracking Reminders, camera, pose guide, ghost overlay, timer, weight beside the photo
Stays out of the way Alignment Lining up two photos, by hand or automatically
Stays out of the way Comparison How fast the same pose from two dates lands side by side
Stays out of the way Design Clutter and polish; a feed, badge or upsell costs a point only when it blocks a check-in or compare
Power tool Performance Import time for a big library, and whether scrolling the full history stutters
Private Privacy App Store label, where photos live, any account, an app lock
Private Backup Whether check-ins survive deleting the app or switching phones
Specialized tool Import (bonus, outside the total) Old photos from the Photos app, past weights, Apple Health

Philosophy and cost get a sentence in each review, not a score. The philosophy is the one thing the developer built around, like Fit-Stitch's one-payment iCloud sync or Check-In's "no AI by design."

The test kit

One kit for every app: [CONFIRM: iPhone model] on iOS [CONFIRM: version], tested [CONFIRM: dates] with no OS update. The set is 12 check-ins [CONFIRM: count] from my archive, each with front, side and back and that day's weight, spanning a cut and a bulk [CONFIRM]. One before/after pair, [CONFIRM: first date] and [CONFIRM: last date], appears in every compare screen.

In each app I:

  1. Installed it fresh, noting the first paywall and any account prompt.
  2. Logged a new check-in with the app's camera, timing and counting taps from the reminder to a side-by-side with last week. My yardstick is under a minute and 5 taps.
  3. Aligned the shared pair with the app's own tools.
  4. Compared the pair side by side and shared it as one image.
  5. Checked storage in settings, the privacy label and airplane mode.
  6. Deleted the app and restored an iPhone backup, then counted what came back [CONFIRM: done for every app].
  7. Imported the 12 check-ins, timing a 100-photo bulk import where possible [CONFIRM: timings].
  8. Loaded my full library [CONFIRM: photo count] and scrolled the full history, noting any stutter. I ran this only on the leading apps, because loading hundreds of photos takes a long time in each.

I bought the cheapest full tier of every app, with no promo codes [CONFIRM: purchases, dates, and Photo Journey's tier]. I couldn't test Android or long-term reliability.

Candidates came from the 15 ranking roundups and from App Store search and charts on Sep 30, 2026. I kept the most-mentioned, best-rated, top-ranked and newest apps, all updated in 2026. Every app I dropped is listed below with the reason.

Apps built for another job are out of scope. The 732-review study

I searched 732 written App Store reviews of seven apps for the features these apps advertise. One regex per feature, then I read every hit and coded it as an ask, a complaint or praise. Reminders and notes get thanked; tags, streaks, themes and feeds get no asks. Caveats: 511 reviews are Lasmit's, and only 159 date from 2024 or later. The full counts, quotes and regexes are in what people want from progress photo apps.

Where the estimates were wrong

The v7 grades were estimates from listings and teardowns, made before hands-on testing. Hands-on, compare and alignment came in 1 to 3 points lower. Estimates held for what a listing shows: whether a feature exists, design polish and price. They missed how fast and good compare and alignment are, behavior with hundreds of photos, and where a feature lives in the app.

Proposed: a measured method

On Oct 4 I drafted a stricter method that scores only measured behavior. It isn't adopted yet. [TODO: Mark decides between this and v8]

  1. Baseline, pass or fail, 8 items: a dated photo saved in the app; several poses per check-in or one album per pose; weight with the photo; any two dates side by side in the same pose; some alignment aid; a reminder; a before/after image export; and the core loop working with no account in airplane mode. An app meets the baseline at 7 of 8.
  2. Six weighted differentiators, 0 to 3 each, out of 100:
Differentiator Weight What I measure
Compare and alignment 30% Seconds from app open to oldest vs newest front side by side, and whether the pair lines up within 60 s
Check-in speed 20% Seconds and taps to a saved 3-pose check-in with weight
Capture consistency 15% Ghost overlay with opacity, pose slots or guide, timer, hands-free or auto-capture
Privacy and data safety 15% Where photos live, account, label, app lock, delete-and-restore result
Scale 10% Import 300 photos (or the app's limit) and scroll the full history
Price 10% Cost per year of the cheapest tier that runs the core loop
  1. Three tiers: Exceptional (baseline met, 70 or more, top 3 on compare or check-in speed, no red flag), Average (40 to 69), and Below average (baseline missed, under 40, or a red flag).
  2. Red flags: photos lost in the restore test, a paywall that hides saved photos or caps them after setup, photos uploaded without opt-in, or a crash or stall at 300 photos.
  3. A 10-minute protocol per app, screen-recorded: install, import a 36-photo kit, a timed check-in, a timed compare, an image export with airplane mode, then the 300-photo scale test.

What I haven't tested yet
