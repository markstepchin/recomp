Import Years of Progress Photos into Recomp at Once

Quick Answer

I built bulk import because I had 450 progress photos in my camera roll, and none of the 10+ apps I tried could import them. Recomp takes an album or a selection of up to 500 photos, groups them into check-ins by the day each photo was taken, and can fill in weights from a CSV. It works on copies, so the originals in your Photos app stay unchanged.

[SCREENSHOT: Import screen with "Choose photos" and "Import an album"]

Pick an album, get dated check-ins

Bulk import turns a pile of photos into dated check-ins without typing a single date. You can choose individual photos for a small batch or import a whole album. The album list shows every album with photos in it, including Favorites and Recents. I keep mine in an album called "progress photos."

Recomp reads each photo's date and groups photos from the same calendar day into one check-in. The first three photos of a day fill the front, side, and back slots in the order you took them. Extra photos from that day and photos with no usable date are skipped and are listed in the summary after you save. Days that already have a check-in are skipped too, so importing the same album twice doesn't create duplicates.

[SCREENSHOT: "Import weights" step with the found dates and the CSV preview]

Weights come in from a CSV file. Recomp lists the dates it found and exports them as a two-column file, Date and Weight. Fill in the weights in Numbers, Excel, or Google Sheets, then import the file back. Recomp shows how many dates matched your photos and how many rows didn't. The step is optional, and Apple Health sync is coming later.

Before anything is saved, you review every check-in. You can swap front, side, and back if a photo landed in the wrong slot, and type in any missing weight. A filter narrows the list to check-ins missing a weight or a photo. Dates come from the photos and can't be changed here.

[SCREENSHOT: Review step with the missing-weight filter open]

This replaces work nobody would do by hand. My 450 photos were 150 weekly check-ins. Entering each one means setting a date, picking three photos, and typing a weight. That adds up to hours and thousands of taps, which is why my photos sat in my camera roll for so long.

Why I spent so much time making it fast

Importing photos is a data-heavy operation. For every photo, Recomp saves a full-resolution copy, a display-size version for the compare timeline, and a thumbnail. A 450-photo import saves 1,350 files to your iPhone. Done all at once after you tap Save, that would mean a long stare at a progress bar.

So Recomp starts part of that work while you're still reviewing. As you add weights and check poses, it builds thumbnails and display versions in the background. Review doesn't wait for it, and every check-in is visible right away. When you tap Save, Recomp copies the originals and finishes the rest behind a progress bar. In one test on an iPhone, about 450 photos took roughly 40 seconds in total, most of it during review. Times vary by phone.

Import is the first thing anyone with years of photos does in Recomp. One of my four principles for the app is top performance across hundreds or thousands of photos. A slow import would break that promise before the timeline ever loads.

What happens to your original photos

Recomp copies your photos and never changes the originals. It only reads from your photo library and saves the copy in its own storage on your iPhone. Your originals, albums, and edits in the Photos app stay as they were. If you edited a photo in Photos, Recomp copies the edited version. Your photos never leave your device.

Each import is limited to 500 photos. An album with more than 500 is rejected as a whole, not partially imported. For a larger library, split it into albums by date range, such as one per year, and import them one at a time. Dates you already imported are skipped, so re-running an album is safe.

Album photos must be downloaded to your iPhone, not stored only in iCloud. With Optimize iPhone Storage turned on, older photos are often iCloud-only. If any photo in an album is iCloud-only, Recomp stops and tells you how many aren't downloaded. Recomp doesn't download them itself, so open them in Photos and try again. This requirement may change later.

Nothing shows up in your timeline until Save finishes. Before it saves, Recomp checks that there is enough free space. If there isn't, or Save fails, or the app closes in the middle, you won't see a half-finished check-in. Leaving the import screen cancels the import.

Questions about bulk import

Does importing change or delete photos in my Photos app?

No. Recomp reads your photos and saves copies in its own storage. Nothing in the Photos app is moved, edited, or deleted.

What format does the weight CSV need?

A header row with a "date" column and a "weight" column. Dates use the YYYY-MM-DD format, and anything after the date, like a time, is ignored. The easiest start is the dates file Recomp exports during import.

How many photos can I import at once?

Up to 500 per import. The photo picker stops at 500, and an album with more than 500 is rejected. Split a larger library into albums and import them one at a time.
