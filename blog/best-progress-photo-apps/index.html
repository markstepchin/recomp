The scorecard

Each app gets 1 to 5 on the five things a weekly photo habit depends on:

  • Notify → capture loop: how easy the weekly check-in is.
  • Compare + align speed: how fast you get a clean side-by-side, and whether you can fix a crooked pose in place.
  • Weight + photo together: whether your weight sits next to the photo.
  • Import and big libraries: whether years of old photos come in, and whether the app copes with hundreds.
  • Clean UI: fewer taps, less clutter.

Tier 1: the free DIY stack

Your phone's Photos app (plus Notes and Reminders)

Start here: it's free, it's already on your phone, and it tells you whether the habit sticks. Make a "Progress" album, set a weekly reminder, and take the same three photos each week. The Hidden album keeps them out of your grid, and iCloud Photos backs them up.

Best for: finding out if you'll keep taking photos before you commit to an app.

[SCREENSHOT: Photos app – progress album grid]

Where it breaks: there's no side-by-side view, so I ended up screenshotting two photos next to each other. Weight lives in a note or spreadsheet, apart from the picture. After a year, finding "week 6" means a lot of scrolling.

Score: 13/25, C [CONFIRM]. Beginner all-in-one.

Tier 2: progress photos inside apps you already use

These apps do something else well and treat photos as a side feature, which is fine if you already live in one.

Hevy

If you log every session in Hevy, the photo feature is free and already there. Photos sit under Measures with body weight, body fat and measurements, and stay private even on a public profile. You can share two photos side by side as one image [CONFIRM all hands-on].

Best for: lifters who want one photo next to their training data and nothing more.

[SCREENSHOT: Hevy – measures entry with photo]

Where it falls short: no alignment, and the side-by-side is a static image. Reports say it allows one photo per day [CONFIRM], which doesn't fit front, side and back. Photos live in your Hevy account, so you need one.

Score: 13/25, C [CONFIRM]. Power-user suite piece.

MyFitnessPal

If you already weigh in on MyFitnessPal, you can attach a photo to the weigh-in, so the number and the picture stay together. For some people that's enough.

Best for: people tracking weight and food in MFP who want a photo on the same entry.

[SCREENSHOT: MyFitnessPal – weigh-in with photo]

Where it falls short: body photos get cropped square [CONFIRM], which cuts off a full-body shot. There's no alignment, and photos sit in your MFP account.

Score: 11/25, C [CONFIRM]. Beginner all-in-one.

Tier 3: dedicated progress photo apps

Most of these do the core job. They differ in alignment, price and where your photos end up.

Body Measurements Tracker App ("Progress" by Lasmit)

Every other list calls it "Progress," but the App Store name is now "Body Measurements Tracker App," and it's the most established app here. It launched in 2012, has 9,355 ratings at 4.63, and ranks first or second on most progress-photo searches in the App Store. It logs measurements, weight, BMI and body fat, puts photos side by side, and syncs both ways with Apple Health.

Best for: measurements and body fat next to your photos, which is probably what you meant if you searched "best body progress tracker app."

[SCREENSHOT: Lasmit Progress – compare view]

Where it falls short: the compare view. Reviewers say it zooms some photos in and others out, with huge borders. A Sep 24, 2026 review says a "lifetime" purchase now needs an annual plan to log more than four measurements, and a Dec 2025 review is titled "Lost FIVE YEARS of data in latest upgrade."

Score: 16/25, B [CONFIRM]. Beginner all-in-one.

Body Progress Pics: Metamorph

Metamorph lines photos up for you and turns them into video. It aligns automatically, overlays the previous shot while you take the next, and exports timelapses, before/after videos and GIFs.

Best for: an aligned timelapse to watch or share.

[SCREENSHOT: Metamorph – compare view]

Where it falls short: price, up to $129.99 for lifetime. The angriest reviews say you can't try it without subscribing [CONFIRM free tier]. It appears to be iPhone only [CONFIRM], and Metamorph's own blog ranks Metamorph first.

GainFrame: Gym Progress Photos (best for AI analysis)

If you want an app to estimate your body fat from photos, use GainFrame. It adds a physique score, 12-muscle-group analysis, an AI coach and "Future You" projections to guided poses, camera-roll import, before/after cards, and Apple Health, Hevy and Strava connections. It has 64 ratings at 4.89, needs no account to start, and its own roundups disclose that GainFrame wrote them.

Best for: anyone who wants numbers on top of their photos.

[SCREENSHOT: GainFrame – AI analysis result]

The trade-offs: GainFrame uploads each photo it analyzes, though the library stays on your phone. AI estimates are also noisy across the category: in another AI physique app, a reviewer posted the same photo twice and got 9.6, then 8.5. Trust two aligned photos more than the number.

Score: 19/25, A [CONFIRM]. Beginner all-in-one.

The best progress photo app for iPhone, Android and each job

What's the best free progress photo app?

Recomp and Check-In are free with no in-app purchases. Recomp runs on iOS 15.1 and up, and Check-In needs iOS 26. Most other "free" apps charge for the features you want, so find the paywall before you import two years of photos.

What's the best app for gym progress photos?

If you already log lifts in Hevy, start with its photo feature, and move to a dedicated app when you want real side-by-side views. For a setup that works in any app, see my guide on how to take progress photos [CONFIRM final guide URL].

What's the best before and after photo app for fitness?

For one before/after, any app with a side-by-side view works, and Metamorph and Photo Journey make the best video version. More on how to compare progress photos side by side [Editor: REC-64 page not live yet; don't link until it is].

What happens to your photos when you switch phones

Lost photos are the second most common complaint in App Store reviews, after paywalls, as the Lasmit, Photo Journey and Snapsie reviews above show. Every app here uses one of three storage models.

1. On your device only. Recomp, Check-In, Thumbstone, Lasmit and Then & Now [CONFIRM] keep photos on your phone. That's the most private option, and only as safe as your phone backup. Whether each app's data comes across in an iCloud or computer backup [CONFIRM per app].

2. Your own iCloud. Fit-Stitch syncs through your iCloud account, Metamorph can store on the device or in iCloud, and the Photos app with iCloud Photos on is the simplest safety net.

3. Someone else's servers. Hevy and MyFitnessPal keep photos in your account on their servers, so signing in on a new phone brings them back. GainFrame is a hybrid: the library stays on your phone, and analyzed photos get uploaded.

Recomp has no export and no backup feature today. There's no way to get check-ins out of the app, and no account or cloud copy to restore from. Whether Recomp's data comes across in an iCloud or encrypted computer backup, and whether originals stay in your Photos library after import [CONFIRM both]. Details are in Recomp's privacy policy.

Whatever you use:

  • Keep the originals in your Photos library.
  • Turn on iCloud backup or an encrypted computer backup.
  • Test a restore before you trade in your old phone.
  • If your app can export, export once a quarter.

FAQ

What is the best app to track progress?

It depends on what you track: Lasmit for measurements and body fat, GainFrame for AI estimates, Then & Now or Thumbstone for a capture-first habit, and Recomp for years of existing photos, free.

What is the best app to track pictures?

For progress photos, a dedicated app beats the Photos app once you want side-by-side views with your weight on screen. The Photos app is a fine place to start.

What is the best way to take progress photos?

Same spot, light, time of day and three poses (front, side, back), once a week. My guide on how to take progress photos covers the setup [CONFIRM final URL].

Is there a free progress photo app with no subscription?

Yes. Recomp and Check-In: Physique are free with no in-app purchases (Check-In needs iOS 26). Fit-Stitch is a one-time $4.99.

Do progress photo apps upload my photos?

Some do. Metamorph, Check-In and Then & Now carry a "Data Not Collected" label. Recomp keeps photos on your device; its label lists anonymous usage analytics and crash reports, none linked to you and none used for tracking. GainFrame uploads the photos it analyzes, and Hevy and MyFitnessPal store photos on their servers.

Can I import years of old progress photos?

Recomp bulk-imports photos into check-ins by date and imports weight from a CSV. Then & Now lists bulk import. Most other apps add photos one at a time [CONFIRM].

Is Recomp the same as Recomp AI?

No. Recomp: Progress Photo Tracker is my progress photo app. "Recomp AI – 3D Body Scanner" is a body scanner from ISO Labs.
