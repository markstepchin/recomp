Why trust me on progress photo apps

I've taken 450+ weekly progress photos [CONFIRM] since [CONFIRM: year], and for years they sat in my camera roll. Every week I shot front, side and back, and logged my weight. iOS Photos stored them but had no side-by-side view, so I built Recomp.

Before opening any app, I read the 15 roundups that rank for this search. Two say they compared advertised features instead of testing. Eight show no screenshots, several still recommend apps last updated in 2017, and most put their own app first. So I don't ask you to trust my job title: every claim below comes with a screenshot from my own test photos.

Who this guide is for

It's for anyone taking weekly physique photos who wants a side-by-side record, starting today or with years of check-ins already in the camera roll. It's not for 3D body scans, which apps like MeThreeSixty handle.

How I picked and tested these apps

Every app got the same photos on the same iPhone, judged on criteria I wrote down before opening any of them. So the differences are the apps, not my lighting or my loyalty.

How I picked

Candidates came from the 15 ranking roundups and from App Store search and charts on Sep 30, 2026. I kept the most-mentioned, best-rated, top-ranked and newest apps, all updated in 2026. I judged capture aids, alignment, where photos are stored, importing old photos, and the paywall. Every app I dropped is in The competition, with the reason.

In each app I:

  1. Installed it fresh, noting the first paywall and any account prompt.
  2. Imported the 12 check-ins, and timed a 100-photo bulk import where one exists [CONFIRM: timings].
  3. Logged one new check-in, counting taps from the reminder to "saved." About 5 taps is the yardstick.
  4. Opened the shared pair in compare and fixed the alignment with the app's own tools.
  5. Tried to get photos back out: originals, video, GIF or share card.
  6. Checked where photos live: settings, privacy label, and airplane mode.

I bought the cheapest tier that unlocks everything and took no promo codes. Each entry names the tier and price [CONFIRM: purchases and dates]. The known tiers come to about $33–39 [CONFIRM: Photo Journey and MyFitnessPal tiers]. Check-In and Recomp have nothing to buy.

What I couldn't test: Android, and long-term reliability. A few weeks of check-ins is not five years.

How the grades work

Each app gets 1 to 5 on the five things a weekly photo habit depends on:

  1. Notify → capture loop: how easy the weekly check-in is.
  2. Compare + align: how fast you get a clean side-by-side, and whether you can fix a crooked pose in place.
  3. Weight + photo: whether your weight sits next to the photo.
  4. Import and big libraries: whether years of old photos come in, and whether the app copes with hundreds.
  5. Clean UI: fewer taps, less clutter.

The total out of 25 becomes a grade, and no total crowns a winner. S (23–25) does the job brilliantly, A (19–22) is strong, B (15–18) works with friction, C (11–14) treats photos as an afterthought, and D (10 or under) I'd avoid [CONFIRM: cut points]. The rubric favors a big library and a weekly habit. That's who I write for, and it also suits my app, so discount accordingly.

Recomp is for the person I was: years of progress photos in the camera roll and no way to see them. What happens to your photos when you switch phones

Lost photos are the second most common complaint in App Store reviews, after paywalls, as the Lasmit, Photo Journey and Snapsie reviews above show. Every app here uses one of three storage models.

1. On your device only. Recomp, Check-In, Thumbstone, Lasmit and Then & Now [CONFIRM] keep photos on your phone. That's the most private option, and only as safe as your phone backup [CONFIRM per app: data survives a restore].

2. Your own iCloud. Fit-Stitch syncs through your iCloud account, and Metamorph can store on the device or in iCloud. The Photos app with iCloud Photos on is the simplest safety net.

3. Someone else's servers. Hevy and MyFitnessPal keep photos in your account, so signing in on a new phone brings them back. GainFrame is a hybrid: the library stays on your phone, and analyzed photos get uploaded.

Recomp has no export and no iCloud backup today. Check-ins can't leave the app, and there's no account or cloud copy to restore from. Your originals stay in the Photos app, so the photos are as safe as that library. Whether Recomp's data survives a device backup is [CONFIRM]. Details are in Recomp's privacy policy.

[SCREENSHOT: Recomp – settings privacy]

Whatever you use:

  1. Keep the originals in your Photos library.
  2. Turn on iCloud backup or an encrypted computer backup.
  3. Test a restore before you trade in your old phone.
  4. If your app can export, export once a quarter.

FAQ

What is the best app to track progress? It depends on what you track: Lasmit for measurements and body fat, GainFrame for AI estimates, Then & Now or Thumbstone for a capture-first habit, and Recomp for years of existing photos, free.

What is the best app to track pictures? For progress photos, a dedicated app beats the Photos app once you want side-by-side views with your weight on screen.

What is the best way to take progress photos? Same spot, light, time of day and three poses (front, side, back), once a week. My guide on how to take progress photos covers the setup [CONFIRM: final URL].

Is there a free progress photo app with no subscription? Yes. Check-In: Physique and Recomp are free with no in-app purchases, though Check-In needs iOS 26. Fit-Stitch is a one-time $4.99.

Do progress photo apps upload my photos? Some do. GainFrame uploads the photos it analyzes, and Hevy and MyFitnessPal store photos on their servers. Check-In, Then & Now and Metamorph carry a "Data Not Collected" label. Recomp keeps photos on your device and collects anonymous analytics and crash data, not linked to you.

Can I import years of old progress photos? Recomp bulk-imports photos into check-ins by date and imports weight from a CSV. Then & Now lists bulk import. Most other apps add photos one at a time [CONFIRM].

Is Recomp the same as Recomp AI? No. Recomp: Progress Photo Tracker is my progress photo app. "Recomp AI – 3D Body Scanner" is a body scanner from ISO Labs.
