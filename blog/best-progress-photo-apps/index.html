Why I wrote this (and how it's different)

Most "best progress photo apps" lists are written by app makers who never opened the other apps. I read 15 of them while researching this. Two say outright that they compared advertised features rather than testing anything. Eight don't show a single screenshot of the apps they rank. Several still recommend apps that haven't been updated since 2017. And almost all of them put their own app at the top, usually without telling you.

I'm a lifter and a software engineer, and I've taken 450+ weekly photos [CONFIRM] over the last few years [CONFIRM: span]. They sat in my camera roll for a long time, buried between screenshots and dog pictures, and I couldn't answer the only question that mattered: am I actually changing? That's why I ended up building an app. It's also why I wanted this list to be useful to someone in the same spot, whether or not they pick my app.

So the rule here is simple: same photos, same check-in, same scorecard, in every app.

The scorecard

Five things matter for a weekly progress photo habit, so every app gets 1 to 5 on each:

  • Notify → capture loop: how easy it is to do the weekly check-in, every week.
  • Compare + align speed: how fast you get a clean side-by-side, and whether you can fix a crooked pose without leaving the compare screen.
  • Weight + photo together: whether your weight sits next to the photo, or lives somewhere else.
  • Import and big libraries: whether you can bring in years of old photos, and whether the app copes once you have hundreds.
  • Clean UI: fewer taps, less clutter.

The total out of 25 becomes a grade: S (23–25) does the job brilliantly, A (19–22) is strong, B (15–18) works with some friction, C (11–14) treats photos as an afterthought, and D (10 or under) I'd avoid. Every app also gets an audience tag: beginner all-in-one or power-user suite piece.

One honest caveat: this rubric rewards people with a big existing photo library and a weekly habit. That's who I'm writing for, and it also suits the app I built. Discount accordingly.

Tier 1: the free DIY stack

Your phone's Photos app (plus Notes and Reminders)

Start here, honestly. It's free, it's already on your phone, and it'll tell you whether the habit sticks. Make an album called "Progress," set a weekly reminder, and take the same three photos every week. If you care about privacy, the Hidden album keeps them out of your main grid, and iCloud Photos backs everything up.

Best for: finding out if you'll actually keep taking photos before you commit to an app.

[SCREENSHOT: Photos app – progress album grid]

Where it breaks: there's no side-by-side view. I ended up screenshotting two photos next to each other, which got old fast. Your weight lives somewhere else (a note, a spreadsheet), so the number and the picture never meet. And after a year, scrolling back to find "week 6" is a chore.

Scorecard: loop 3 · compare 1 · weight 2 · import 4 · UI 3 = 13, C [CONFIRM]. Beginner all-in-one.

Tier 2: progress photos inside apps you already use

These apps do something else well and offer progress photos on the side. That's fine if you're already living in one of them. Just know the photos are a second-class citizen.

Hevy

If you log every session in Hevy, the photo feature is free and already there. Photos live under Measures, alongside body weight, body fat and measurements, and they stay private even if your profile is public. You can put two photos side by side and share the result as one image [CONFIRM all hands-on].

Best for: lifters who want one photo next to their training data and nothing more.

[SCREENSHOT: Hevy – measures entry with photo]

Where it falls short: there's no alignment, and the side-by-side is static. Reports say it's one photo per day [CONFIRM], which doesn't fit front/side/back. Your photos live in your Hevy account rather than on your phone, so you need an account.

Scorecard: loop 2 · compare 2 · weight 4 · import 1 · UI 4 = 13, C [CONFIRM]. Power-user suite piece.

MyFitnessPal

If you already weigh in on MyFitnessPal, you can attach a photo to the weigh-in, so the number and the picture stay together. That's the whole pitch, and for some people it's enough.

Best for: people tracking weight and food in MFP who want a photo on the same entry.

[SCREENSHOT: MyFitnessPal – weigh-in with photo]

Where it falls short: body photos get cropped square [CONFIRM], which mangles a full-body shot. There's no alignment. The photos sit in your MFP account. It works because you're already there, not because it was built for this.

Scorecard: loop 2 · compare 2 · weight 4 · import 1 · UI 2 = 11, C [CONFIRM]. Beginner all-in-one.

Tier 3: dedicated progress photo apps

This is where the real choice is. Most of these do the core job; the differences are in how they line photos up, what they charge, and where your photos end up.

Body Measurements Tracker App ("Progress" by Lasmit)

Every other list calls this "Progress," but on the App Store it's now "Body Measurements Tracker App," and it's the most established app here. It's been around since 2012, it has 9,355 ratings at 4.63, and it ranks first or second on most progress-photo searches in the App Store. It logs measurements, weight, BMI and body fat, puts photos side by side, and syncs both ways with Apple Health. The listing says your photos stay on your device.

Best for: people who want measurements and body fat logged alongside their photos. If you searched "best body progress tracker app," this is probably what you meant.

[SCREENSHOT: Lasmit Progress – compare view]

Where it falls short: the compare view is the weak spot. Reviewers complain that it zooms some photos in and others out, and that the borders are huge. Pricing has annoyed long-time users: one Sep 2026 review says a "lifetime" purchase now needs an annual plan to log more than four measurements. Another review from Dec 2025 is titled "Lost FIVE YEARS of data in latest upgrade." Back up whatever you use.

Price: free, with plans from $3.99 a month, $11.99–19.99 a year, or $29.99 lifetime. Privacy label: Data Not Linked to You. Updated: Sep 22, 2026.

Best for: anyone who wants numbers on top of their photos.

What happens to your photos when you switch phones

Losing photos is the second most common complaint I found in App Store reviews, after paywalls. People lost five years in an update, or 53 days on a new phone, or everything after choosing the wrong storage setting. So before you pick, know which of three models you're signing up for.

1. On your device only. Recomp, Check-In, Thumbstone, Lasmit and Then & Now [CONFIRM] keep your photos on your phone. That's the most private option, but your photos are only as safe as your phone backup. I don't have an in-app export or backup in Recomp today. Whether each app's data comes across in an iCloud or computer backup [CONFIRM per app]. For Recomp specifically, whether originals stay in your Photos library after import, and whether Recomp's data is included in your backup [CONFIRM both].

2. Your own iCloud. Fit-Stitch syncs through your iCloud account. Metamorph can store on the device or in iCloud. And the Photos app with iCloud Photos turned on is the simplest safety net of all.

3. Someone else's servers. Hevy and MyFitnessPal keep photos in your account, so they come back when you sign in on a new phone, but they live on the company's servers. GainFrame is a hybrid: the library is on your phone, but photos you analyze get uploaded.

[SCREENSHOT: Recomp – settings privacy]

Whatever you choose, do these four things:

  • Keep the originals in your Photos library.
  • Turn on iCloud backup or an encrypted computer backup.
  • Test a restore before you trade in your old phone.
  • If your app can export, export once a quarter.

What to look for in a progress photo app

Five questions sort out almost every app on this list:

  1. Where do my photos live, and does anything get uploaded? Read the App Store privacy label.
  2. When does it start charging? Check this before you import anything.
  3. How does it line photos up? The options are a ghost overlay when you shoot, automatic alignment afterward, manual adjustment, or nothing.
  4. Can it take my old photos, with the right dates?
  5. When was it last updated?

If you want a free progress photo app that's built around the last two, that's what I made Recomp for.

FAQ

What is the best app to track progress?

It depends on what you track. For measurements and body fat, use Lasmit's Body Measurements Tracker. For AI estimates, use GainFrame. For a capture-first photo habit, use Then & Now or Thumbstone. For organizing years of existing photos for free, use Recomp.

What is the best app to track pictures?

For progress photos specifically, a dedicated app beats the Photos app once you want side-by-side comparisons and your weight on the same screen. The Photos app is still a fine place to start.

What is the best way to take progress photos?

Use the same spot, the same light, the same time of day and the same three poses (front, side, back), once a week. My full guide on how to take progress photos covers setup [CONFIRM final URL].

Is there a free progress photo app with no subscription?

Yes. Recomp and Check-In: Physique are free with no in-app purchases (Check-In needs iOS 26). Fit-Stitch is a one-time $4.99.

Do progress photo apps upload my photos?

Some do. Apps with a "Data Not Collected" label (Recomp, Metamorph, Check-In, Then & Now) say they don't collect data. AI apps like GainFrame upload the photos they analyze, and account-based apps like Hevy and MyFitnessPal store photos on their servers.

Can I import years of old progress photos?

Recomp bulk-imports and groups photos into check-ins by date, and Then & Now lists bulk import. Most other apps make you add photos one at a time [CONFIRM].

Is Recomp the same as Recomp AI?

No. Recomp: Progress Photo Tracker is the progress photo app I make. "Recomp AI – 3D Body Scanner" is a body-scanning app from a different company (ISO Labs).
