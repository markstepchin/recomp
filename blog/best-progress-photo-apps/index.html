Why trust me on progress photo apps

I've taken 450+ weekly progress photos [CONFIRM] since [CONFIRM: year], and for years they sat in my camera roll. Each week I shot front, side and back and logged my weight. iOS Photos had no side-by-side view, so I built Recomp.

First, I read the 15 roundups that rank for this search. Two compared listed features without testing. Eight show no screenshots, several recommend apps last updated in 2017, and most put their own app first. I put mine first too, so every claim below has a screenshot from my own photos and a row in one table.

What I tested, and what's out of scope

I graded every app on the jobs a weekly photo habit depends on. Each app entry covers them in this order, with a screenshot of that app's approach to each:

  1. Capture and tracking: camera, pose guide, ghost overlay, timer, reminders, and weight beside the photo.
  2. Alignment: how a crooked or zoomed photo gets lined up, by hand or automatically. I tested how reliable automatic alignment is in does auto-alignment work on progress photos? [CONFIRM: live before publish; final URL].
  3. Comparison: how fast the same pose from two dates ends up side by side.
  4. Privacy: the App Store privacy label and where the photos live.
  5. Backup: whether check-ins survive deleting the app or switching phones.
  6. Bonus, import and integrations: old photos from the Photos app, past weights, and Apple Health.

Each entry also names the app's philosophy and what it costs. The philosophy is the one thing the developer built around, like Fit-Stitch's one-payment iCloud sync or Check-In's "no AI by design." Cost covers the price, what sits behind the paywall, and whether you need an account.

Three considerations cut across every feature:

  1. Design and usability: taps per check-in, clutter and overall polish.
  2. Pointless features: a feed, badge or upsell costs a point only when it blocks a check-in or compare.
  3. Performance under load: how long a [CONFIRM: photo count]-photo import takes, and whether scrolling the full history stutters.

This guide is for weekly physique photos, whether you start today or have years of them in the camera roll. Apps built for another job are out of scope:

  1. Calorie trackers like MyFitnessPal, which attach a photo to a weigh-in.
  2. Workout trackers and plan generators like Hevy, which file photos under body measures.
  3. AI body-fat analysis apps like GainFrame. What scans and AI can tell you covers their accuracy.
  4. Collage and timelapse makers, which turn photos into a video or a layout, not a record.
  5. 3D body scanners like MeThreeSixty.

GainFrame, Hevy, MyFitnessPal and timelapse makers each get a line in The competition.

What scans and AI can tell you about your body in 2026

Phone scans and AI body-fat apps repeat themselves closely but miss lab methods by roughly 2 to 6 points. In a 2026 study, MeThreeSixty, Bodygram and ZOZOFIT all read lower than DXA, and none matched it within 2 points. I compare them with DEXA, BIA scales, calipers and the Navy tape method in How accurate are AI body-fat estimates? [CONFIRM: live before publish; REC-80 is still draft PR 19].

No study I found tests whether an app can tell flat from full, bloated or holding water. Validation studies remove those states on purpose: participants fast and skip training first. Amazon's paper lists a large meal, an intense workout and flexing as possible causes of error. Even DXA, the reference, read 2 to 3% more lean mass in 18 cyclists after 48 hours of carb loading (3% when combined with creatine loading), though they'd gained almost no muscle.

What's necessary is the same photos, taken the same way, every week. Every number above needed fixed pose, light, distance and timing. In a 74-person study, consistent weeks of weigh-ins predicted less regain, and the total count didn't. A ghost overlay or auto-alignment helps you keep that routine. Physique scores and AI projections don't change next week's plan [CONFIRM: Mark agrees with this fluff call]. Recomp has a reminder, a ghost of your last photo per pose [after 3.6: ghost overlay] and two dates side by side, but no auto-alignment.

The goal is to learn to read your own body, not to outsource it to AI. Jeff Nippard's What Every Body Fat % Actually Looks Like (50% to 5%) shows DEXA-measured people at the same percentage looking very different. I was bad at reading my own photos at first, and what fixed it was years of weekly side-by-sides, not a number [CONFIRM]. I cover telling flat from bloated from leaner in [LINK: separate article on reading progress photos], and why I skip AI in [LINK: separate article on reading your own body vs AI].

Recomp skips all of these except System, Light and Dark appearance, and it covers the sharing need. Its share button builds a new before/after image and sends it through the share sheet, to a coach or anyone else. It has no feed, widget or Watch app.

Timelapses and collages belong to a different kind of app. Only 6 of the 732 reviews mention a timelapse or video, and none mention a collage. One asks for a timelapse export, one wants a broken progress video fixed, and two praise one. The other two complain about a low-resolution, watermarked video and an app that "only allows you to take photos and export them as a “time lapse”" with no side-by-side. Compare and side-by-side terms appear in 67 reviews.

Two of these apps started as timelapse makers. Metamorph began as a stop-motion app for renovations, plants and kids, and Photo Journey as a daily timelapse app, before both turned to body progress. Three Fit-Stitch reviews from 2014 to 2016 discuss its before/after layouts, and one asks it to "go back to the old before/after set up." I don't grade timelapse or collage output here.

AI body fat is wanted, and accuracy is the problem. Five reviews praise an app's AI analysis, two of them its body-fat number. Seven more complain that a measurement tracker won't calculate body fat from tape or caliper numbers. What scans and AI can tell you covers why I skip it.

Measurements are a real need, for a different audience. In the measurement tracker, 220 of 511 reviews mention measurements, more than mention photos (125). In the six photo-first apps, 2 of 221 reviews ask for them. Recomp had none, so this roundup sends tape-measure trackers to Lasmit. Recomp 3.6 adds eight optional measurements so those lifters don't pass it over [after 3.6: body measurements]. It's an honest bet on that audience, not something the reviews asked for.

Reminders are the feature people thank apps for. Of the 37 reviews that mention reminders, 32 are positive. The 5 complaints are about reminders that don't fire, ignore the chosen day, or can't be set the way people want. Recomp has a weekly reminder.

Phases have no demand yet. One app groups check-ins into bulk and cut blocks, and none of the 732 reviews mention bulk, cut, deload or a GLP-1 drug. I'll build them only once readers ask [CONFIRM: Mark's call on phases]. If you'd use them, email me.

The test kit

One kit for every app: [CONFIRM: iPhone model] on iOS [CONFIRM: version], tested [CONFIRM: dates] with no OS update. The set is 12 check-ins [CONFIRM: count] from my archive, each with front, side and back shots and that day's weight, spanning a cut and a bulk [CONFIRM]. In each app I:

  1. Installed it fresh, noting the first paywall and any account prompt.
  2. Logged a new check-in with the app's camera, counting taps from reminder to "saved" (yardstick: 5).
  3. Aligned the shared pair with the app's own tools.
  4. Compared the pair side by side and shared it as one image.
  5. Checked storage in settings, the privacy label and airplane mode.
  6. Deleted the app and restored an iPhone backup, then counted what came back [CONFIRM: done for every app].
  7. Imported the 12 check-ins, timing a 100-photo bulk import where possible [CONFIRM: timings].
  8. Loaded [CONFIRM: photo count] photos and scrolled the full history, noting any stutter.

I bought the cheapest full tier of every app, with no promo codes [CONFIRM: new total without GainFrame and Hevy; purchases, dates and Photo Journey's tier]. I couldn't test Android or long-term reliability.

How the grades work

Six scores of 1 to 5 make a grade out of 30: capture and tracking, alignment, comparison, privacy, backup, and design and performance. Import and integrations gets its own 1 to 5 as a bonus, shown in the summary table but kept out of the total. S is 28–30, A 23–27, B 18–22, C 13–17, and D 12 or under [CONFIRM: cut points].

Import stays out of the total because it's the feature I built Recomp around. Counting it would lift my own app's grade. Philosophy and cost get a sentence in each entry, not a score. The best progress photo app for iPhone

Top pick: Recomp: Progress Photo Tracker

Reminder: this is my app. Same test kit, same table, and its flaws are listed below.

Recomp wins because it's a free progress photo app with no account that covers capture, weight, compare and old photos [after 3.6: in-app camera]. The only other free app with no in-app purchases, Check-In, needs iOS 26. Of the 9 apps, only Recomp groups an imported library into check-ins by date [CONFIRM after testing; Then & Now's bulk import may qualify].

The reminder opens straight into the camera or the library, whichever you used last [after 3.6: reminder opens last-used source]. The camera, front or rear, walks you through front, side and back poses [after 3.6: in-app camera]. Your last photo for that pose shows as a ghost overlay, and its opacity slider keeps your setting [after 3.6: ghost overlay]. One check-in can mix camera and library photos [after 3.6: mixed sources].

[SCREENSHOT: Recomp – camera with ghost overlay] [after 3.6: in-app camera, ghost overlay]

Only the date is required, so a week with just a weight or one photo still saves [after 3.6: date-only check-ins]. You can add neck, shoulders, chest, flexed arm, waist, hips, thigh and calf, one side only [after 3.6: body measurements]. Weight and measurements each get their own imperial or metric setting [after 3.6: separate units].

Bulk import turns a camera-roll archive into check-ins. It groups photos by calendar day, up to 3 a day, and a Photos collection comes in with one tap. Past weigh-ins import from a CSV with YYYY-MM-DD dates and a unit picker [after 3.6: CSV unit picker].

[SCREENSHOT: Recomp – bulk import grouped by date]

The compare timeline puts two dates side by side and scrolls to any date instantly. You pan, zoom and rotate a crooked photo in place. Share comparison saves the pair as one JPG, aligned for the pose shown, with each photo's weight and date underneath and no watermark, and opens the iOS share sheet so you can send it to a coach. Your photos never leave your device unless you share them, and a Face ID lock keeps the app closed to anyone else using your phone [after 3.6: Face ID lock].

[SCREENSHOT: Recomp – compare view]

Flaws but not dealbreakers:

  1. No auto-alignment. You straighten photos by hand; Thumbstone and Metamorph do it for you. Even apps that ship it let you turn it off or fix it by hand; Lasmit made its auto-align off by default this year. See how well auto-alignment works [CONFIRM: live before publish; final URL].
  2. No voice control. The camera has a 3- or 10-second timer for each pose [after 3.6: camera timer], but Then & Now adds a voice timer and body silhouettes.
  3. No AI body-fat estimates, timelapses or collages, by choice. Those are separate kinds of apps, out of scope here.
  4. No photo backup or export, and no iCloud sync. Camera shots live only in Recomp and your iPhone backup [after 3.6: camera saves]. Dates and weights export as CSV. Fit-Stitch syncs to your own iCloud. See what happens when you switch phones.
  5. No Apple Health, and measurements don't show in Compare or charts [after 3.6: body measurements]. Lasmit has Apple Health.
  6. A weaker privacy label than Check-In's: anonymous usage analytics and crash reports, not linked to you, no tracking.
  7. Bulk import stops at 500 photos. Bigger albums, and albums with originals only in iCloud, get rejected.
  8. iPhone only, with 0 ratings, so there's no track record yet.

What I'm building next, including an optional private iCloud backup and Apple Health weight, is on the Recomp roadmap. Nothing on it is scored here. What happens to your photos when you switch phones

Lost photos are the No. 2 complaint in App Store reviews, after paywalls, as the Lasmit, Photo Journey and Snapsie reviews show. Every app uses one of three storage models:

  1. On your device only: Recomp, Check-In, Thumbstone and Lasmit [CONFIRM]. Most private, and only as safe as your phone backup [CONFIRM per app: data survives a restore].
  2. Your own iCloud: Fit-Stitch, Metamorph and Then & Now if you turn it on [CONFIRM: Then & Now], and the Photos app with iCloud Photos on.
  3. Someone else's servers: Hevy and MyFitnessPal, so signing in on a new phone brings photos back. GainFrame uploads only the photos it analyzes.

Recomp has no account or cloud sync, so your iPhone backup is the only backup of your check-ins. iCloud and Finder backups include Recomp's check-ins and photos, and restoring one to a new phone brings them back. Imported originals also stay unchanged in Photos. Camera shots exist only inside Recomp and aren't saved to Photos [after 3.6: camera saves]. Delete the app without a recent backup and they're gone. Check-in dates and weights export as a CSV, without photos, from Settings > Advanced Settings. Details are in Recomp's privacy policy.

[SCREENSHOT: Recomp – settings privacy]

Whatever you use:

  1. Keep the originals in your Photos library.
  2. Turn on iCloud or encrypted computer backup.
  3. Test a restore before trading in your old phone.
  4. If your app can export, export quarterly.

FAQ

What is the best app to track progress? For progress photos, Recomp, the free app I built. For measurement history and Apple Health, Lasmit, since Recomp doesn't chart measurements or read Apple Health [after 3.6: body measurements]. I don't grade AI body-fat apps such as GainFrame; what scans and AI can tell you explains why.

What is the best way to take progress photos? Same spot, light, time and three poses, weekly. A ghost overlay of your last photo helps, and Check-In, Then & Now, Photo Journey and Recomp have one [after 3.6: ghost overlay]. My guide on how to take progress photos covers the setup [CONFIRM: final URL].

Is there a free progress photo app with no subscription? Yes. Recomp and Check-In: Physique are free with no in-app purchases, though Check-In needs iOS 26. Fit-Stitch is a one-time $4.99.

Do progress photo apps upload my photos? GainFrame uploads the photos it analyzes, and Hevy and MyFitnessPal store them on their servers. Check-In, Then & Now and Metamorph are labeled "Data Not Collected." Recomp keeps photos on your device and collects anonymous analytics and crash data, not linked to you.

Can I import years of old progress photos? Recomp bulk-imports up to 500 photos at a time into check-ins by date, plus weight from a CSV. Then & Now lists bulk import. Most others add photos one at a time [CONFIRM].

Which progress photo app features matter most? Reminders and a place for notes, judging by 732 App Store reviews. Nobody in them asked for tags, streaks, themes or a social feed, and only 6 mention a timelapse.

Is Recomp the same as Recomp AI? No. "Recomp AI – 3D Body Scanner" is a body scanner from ISO Labs, not my progress photo app.
