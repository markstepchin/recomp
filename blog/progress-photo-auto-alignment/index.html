How progress photo auto-alignment works, and where it fails

I’m building auto-align into Recomp and testing it now, so I have a stake in this topic. Today, Recomp lines up photos by hand. Finding a body in a photo turned out to be the easy part. The hard part is knowing when the answer is wrong, before a bad alignment fakes progress. I checked every quote from other apps below on October 2, 2026.

Why is a body harder to align than a face?

Alignment has to hold the camera constant while your body changes. A smaller waist or a wider back is the change you want to see. Over years, everything around you drifts too: rooms, phones, lighting and mirror distance. Apple’s own image-alignment sample says “The best alignment occurs when the content of the input images is very similar.” Two check-ins a year apart aren’t similar, and that’s the point of taking them.

Faces have a stable anchor, and bodies don’t. AgeLapse aligns “every photo on your eyes,” which suits a selfie timelapse. On a body photo, the head turns on its own, and a back photo has no face.

Three techniques share one label

  1. Image registration lines up the background. It works with a tripod in one room and breaks in a new one.
  2. Body-pose landmarks line up joints like shoulders and hips. Side photos, loose clothing and hidden hips throw them off.
  3. Face detection lines up the eyes. It fails on back photos and tilted heads.

Getting body points out of a photo is no longer the barrier. Apple’s Vision finds up to 19 body points on an iPhone, and Google’s MediaPipe finds 33. Apple lists its own weak spots: people “bent over or upside down” and “obstructive flowing clothing.”

Where auto-alignment goes wrong

It has to pick a reference. Progress Pics by Thumbstone Labs matches “your previous shots.” Chaining each photo to the last is easy to build, but small errors add up, so photo 200 can drift from photo 1. One fixed reference avoids drift, but a new room then breaks every photo after it.

It has to know when it’s wrong. A wrong alignment looks like a result, not a bug. On a side photo, one hip hides behind the other, and the model can grab the wrong one. Lasmit turned its Smart Overlay off by default in version 2026.6 (February 11, 2026), because “when it didn’t work, it was causing a lot of confusion.”

It has to hand control back fast. FormLapse 1.3 added “Fine-tune any photo whose auto-alignment was off,” along with a “Review alignment” list. Thumbstone promises “no manual lining up.” In my test, its auto-align landed about three photos in four, and fixing the rest by hand felt sluggish.

It has to survive big imports and updates. ProgressCam lets you “batch-align in one go,” which is where one quiet error spreads across hundreds of photos. GainFrame’s 2.32 update (July 22, 2026) opens with “PHOTO ALIGNMENT RESTORED.” For a while, photos weren’t aligned to the pose reference at all.

Who handles failure best?

AgeLapse, a face app, tells you when it fails and lets your fix win. Its docs flag photos marked “No Faces Found” or “Failed.” Its README says “A saved manual edit takes precedence over the automatic alignment.” Body apps publish far less. One Lasmit reviewer says its photo feature “zooms in on some pictures and out on others making it hard to actually compare” (4★, July 2025).

How I’m approaching it in Recomp

The version I’m testing fits each photo to your shoulders and hips, on your iPhone. It uses Apple’s Vision, and no photo is uploaded. It refuses a photo tilted more than 25°, one where it can’t find both shoulders and both hips, and one where the torso or shoulder width looks wrong. Every refusal shows the same message, “Couldn’t find your pose,” and leaves that photo for you to fix by hand.

Your fix has to win, so nothing saves until you confirm it. The test build opens each photo in an editor with Reset and Align, and closing with changes asks before discarding them. The original photo is never changed. Until it ships, you line up photos by hand, as described on the Align page.

What’s still unresolved

Nobody publishes how often their auto-alignment is wrong. None of the apps above says how often it fails on side or back photos, or how far a chain drifts over years.
