I build a manual-only app, so here's how I kept this fair

I build Recomp, a progress-photo app for iPhone. Recomp aligns manually (pan, zoom, rotate) and has no auto-align. That makes this test a conflict of interest. I'm scoring auto-align against my own manual alignment, and a fail makes my app look better.

Why is a body photo harder to align than a selfie?

Alignment has to hold the camera constant while the body changes. The change is what you want to see: a smaller waist, a wider back. The camera's distance, height and tilt should match between dates. A tool that lines up the wrong thing can hide real change or create fake change.

Over years, almost everything around the body changes too. My photos span [CONFIRM: years], [CONFIRM: number of rooms], [CONFIRM: phones and lenses] and [CONFIRM: mirror vs. timer shots]. Apple's own image-alignment sample says "The best alignment occurs when the content of the input images is very similar." Two check-ins a year apart aren't similar. That's the point of taking them.

What can "auto-align" mean?

Apps use three different techniques under one label. Each anchors on something different, so each should break in a different place. The last column is my prediction, which the test checks.

Technique What it lines up What it ignores Where I expect it to break
Image registration The pixels of the whole frame, mostly background The body A new room, a moved camera
Body-pose landmarks Shoulders and hips found by a model The background Side photos, loose clothing, hidden hips
Face detection The eyes The torso Back photos, a tilted head

Image registration aligns the scene, not you. Apple's Vision framework offers translational registration, which solves a shift only. Its homographic version solves a perspective warp, which can also bend the body. With a tripod in one room, the background stands in for the camera. In a new room, it doesn't.

Body-pose landmarks anchor on joints. A pose model finds points like shoulders and hips, and a fit scales, rotates and shifts each photo to line them up. Apple's model finds up to 19 points, and Google's MediaPipe finds 33. Apple lists its weak spots: people "bent over or upside down" and "obstructive flowing clothing." Subjects "close to the edges of the screen" also do worse.

Face detection anchors on the eyes. That suits selfie timelapses. AgeLapse aligns "every photo on your eyes." On a body photo, the head turns on its own, and a back photo has no face.

Apple's 3D pose needs depth that old photos don't have to measure real size, so I left it out.

How I tested it

My manual alignment is the answer key. Recomp keeps the pan, zoom and rotation I set for each photo, so every photo already has an alignment I accepted. Each technique produces its own alignment, and I measure the gap between the two. Photos I never adjusted are left out, because I can't tell "already lined up" from "never touched." That excluded [RESULT: number] photos.

The gap is measured in torso lengths. For each photo, I take the shoulder and hip points and compare where my alignment and the tool's alignment put them. The distance is divided by my torso length, from the shoulder line to the hip line. On me, 0.05 torso lengths is about [RESULT: cm], roughly [RESULT: plain-language comparison].

The pass line comes from my own consistency. Before the run, I re-aligned 30 random photos without looking at my first attempt. My two attempts differed by [RESULT: torso lengths], and that spread sets the bar if it's larger than 0.05. A photo passes if the tool lands within that bar, within 2° of rotation and within 3% of scale. A technique "works" if 90% of photos pass in every pose.

Everything ran offline on my Mac. Apple Vision and RTMPose both run locally. MediaPipe keeps images on the device, but Google's notice says its Tasks APIs "send metrics about the performance and utilization of the APIs" to Google. So I downloaded every model first and ran the whole test with the network off [CONFIRM: run done with network off]. No photo left the Mac.

Methods (for developers)

Data. [CONFIRM: count] photos from [CONFIRM: count] check-ins, each tagged front, side or back with its date. The answer key is the manual alignment Recomp stores per photo: a similarity transform (uniform scale, rotation, shift) on a 9:16 canvas. It was exported from my phone to my Mac. Photos with an untouched (identity) adjustment are excluded.

Techniques and versions.

  • Apple Vision, legacy API: VNTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest and VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest (iOS 11+, macOS 10.13+), returning alignmentTransform and warpTransform (docs, docs). VNDetectHumanBodyPoseRequest (iOS 14+, macOS 11+, 19 points). VNDetectFaceLandmarksRequest for the eyes-only baseline (docs).
  • Apple Vision, Swift API (iOS 18+, macOS 15+): DetectHumanBodyPoseRequest, and registration as TrackTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest / TrackHomographicImageRegistrationRequest, run through TargetedImageRequestHandler on two images.
  • 3D pose, not used: VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest (iOS 17+, macOS 14+) returns 17 joints in meters for the most prominent person. It uses depth when present. Without LiDAR capture, bodyHeight returns "a reference height of 1.8 meters" (article).
  • MediaPipe Pose Landmarker: BlazePose GHUM, lite/full/heavy bundles, 33 landmarks (guide); mediapipe 1.0.1, released Aug 14, 2026 (PyPI). Run fully offline (see above).
  • RTMPose through rtmlib 0.0.16 (Aug 2026), on ONNX Runtime with no mmcv (repo). The RTMPose paper reports RTMPose-m at 75.8% AP on COCO with 90+ FPS on an Intel i7-11700 CPU. On the authors' COCO-SinglePerson set it reports 82.4 AP vs. 35.4 for BlazePose-Full. Those are the authors' 2023 numbers on strangers, not alignment of one person over years. MMPose's last release is 1.3.2 (July 2024).

Fit. Pose techniques fit a least-squares similarity transform on shoulders and hips. Side photos use the visible-side shoulder and hip plus [CONFIRM: third point]. Registration uses Vision's own output.

Error. Map each photo's shoulder and hip points through the tool's transform and through the manual one. Take the mean distance, divided by torso length in the manual frame. Points are detected with a different model than the one that aligned, so a pose model never grades itself. "p90" means the error that 90% of photos fall under. "p95" for runtime is the same idea at 95%.

Pass/fail, locked [CONFIRM: date]. Bar T = max(0.05 torso lengths, my 95th-percentile disagreement with myself over 30 blind re-alignments). Per photo: detection found, error ≤ T, rotation within 2°, scale within 3%. A technique works if ≥ 90% pass in every pose and its median error ≤ T. It partly works if ≥ 90% pass in at least one pose.

Reference modes. Fixed: my earliest manually aligned photo per pose. Chained: the previous check-in.

Runtime and cost. Seconds per photo (median, p95) and the total, on [CONFIRM: Mac model, macOS version]. $0 marginal cost.

Published artifacts. [CONFIRM: repo URL]: harness code and a CSV with photo index, date, pose, technique, error, rotation and scale deltas, pass flag and runtime. No photos and no weights.
