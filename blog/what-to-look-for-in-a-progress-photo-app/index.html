What to look for in a progress photo app

Five photos work fine in any progress photo app. An app only matters once you have the habit, with dozens or hundreds of photos, and that person is who it should be built for. It should be a good photos app and a good fitness app, with almost nothing in the way between them.

I build Recomp, so weigh my opinions with that in mind. Where Recomp falls short of something on this page, I say so.

Why I have opinions about this

I've taken weekly progress photos for years, and most apps couldn't handle my library. Working with a coach left me with hundreds of front, side and back photos, and I wrote about why I built Recomp because nothing fit. Before building it, I downloaded more than 10 dedicated apps and paid for a few subscriptions. None stuck.

In October 2026 I tested the category again, end to end. It changed how I see these apps. Most feel like a minimal first version, and only a few look like someone put real care into them. That gap, more than any feature list, is what this page is about.

My principles

These come from building Recomp, but you can hold any app to them.

  1. Tracking takes under a minute and a few taps. Past that, consistency drops, and consistency is the whole point of progress photos.
  2. The app stays out of the way. You're there to see your photos, not my buttons.
  3. It stays fast across hundreds or thousands of photos. The person with a big library is the real user, not an edge case.
  4. It does one job well. It should sit next to Hevy and MyFitnessPal in your routine instead of trying to replace them.
  5. It works without an account. Body photos are personal. Recomp never uploads your photos. It sends anonymous usage analytics and crash reports, with no account and no tracking.
  6. Motion is subtle and native to iOS. The app should feel like it came with your iPhone.
  7. Success means people stick with it. I want Recomp to be the best tool for people who take this seriously. I measure that by whether people keep coming back each week, not by what the app charges.

A progress photo app is a photos app first

Most of the work in a photos app is the part nobody notices. A gallery sounds easy to build. In practice it comes with a mountain of small decisions that you only notice when one is wrong: smooth zoom and swiping, loading states, a filmstrip to jump between photos, dark mode. Those details separate an app you keep from one you delete in the first session. Recomp follows your iPhone's light or dark setting, or you can pick one in Settings.

It has to stay fast with a real library

Speed is where good-looking apps fall apart. GainFrame is the most polished app I tested, with great colors, spacing and loading states. Its bulk import took about 15 minutes and stopped at my latest 250 photos, so most of my history never made it in. I haven't published speed numbers for Recomp yet. Until I do, judge any app, mine included, by loading your own library into it and scrolling.

Picking two photos should take seconds

The photo picker is where I lose the most patience. In GainFrame, choosing a before or after photo opens a grid with thumbnails about a third of the screen tall. With hundreds of photos, you can't really find a specific one. In Recomp, you tap the date under either photo and drag a ruler with one tick per check-in, drawn over your weight chart. Each check-in you pass gives a small haptic tap, stronger on photos you've liked, and the photo changes as you drag.

The photos come first, not the features

An app can be beautiful and still miss the job. In GainFrame, photo comparison comes second, and the UI is built around AI first. Photos sit in small boxes with AI labels over them. My verdict: great polish, but at the core it doesn't do the job for me. Polish is easy to copy. A photo you can't find or see clearly is the bigger problem.

More than one way to look

People want to see their progress in different ways, and the app should let them. Across the category, the same modes keep coming up: side by side, an overlay with an opacity slider, a wipe slider, timelapse, and photos stacked vertically. Stacked never works for me. I'll build my preferred view first and offer the others, so people can look at their progress however they like. Today Recomp shows side by side only, which is a real gap. Thumbstone's Progress Pics has every one of those modes.

It's also a fitness app

Make the fitness decisions for me

You shouldn't have to design a system before your first check-in. A progress photo app should decide the poses, the framing and how photos line up. Recomp gives each check-in a front, side and back slot. You line photos up by dragging, pinching and rotating them right in Compare, with an alignment grid you can turn on, and your original photo never changes.

Recomp doesn't have its own camera yet. You take photos with the iPhone camera and add them from your library.

Auto-alignment is the biggest piece I'm missing. I'm building it and testing it now. Thumbstone's auto-align landed about three photos in four [CONFIRM: Mark's "75%" wording] in my test, a good first pass, though my shirtless photos probably helped.

A check-in is one unit

Photos from the same morning belong together. In Recomp, a check-in is one date with its photos, weight and notes, and later measurements. Many apps keep separate comparison albums for front, back, side and biceps instead, so three photos from one session end up in three unrelated places. Every app also organizes differently, and each new pattern is one more thing to learn before you see any progress.

Weight belongs next to the photo

Your weight should sit under the photo, not in a separate app. Before Recomp, I tried a setup with iOS Photos, weight in Notes, and Calendar reminders. It worked until I wanted to compare, because nothing put the two together. In Recomp, each compared photo shows its date and weight, and a check-in without a weight shows no weight rather than a zero. That lets you put two dates at the same weight side by side, which tells you something the scale can't.

Comparing is what brings you back

The comparison is the payoff, so it should take no effort to reach. The loop I wanted was a Monday morning notification, a photo and a weigh-in, then a look at last week. Reminders start the loop, and the comparison is what makes you do it again next Monday. Recomp sends a weekly reminder on the day and time you choose. Its share button turns the comparison into one image with both dates and weights, ready to send to a coach.

Friction is why people quit

Adding photos takes too many taps and too much thinking in most apps. Most people probably see a mess and quit. Every app lays things out differently, so you have to work out where the buttons and inputs are and what each one means. I gave up on some apps right away because they didn't look like quality work.

The worst friction is getting your existing photos in. None of the apps I first tried could import mine, and adding 450 photos one by one would have taken hours and thousands of taps. Recomp imports up to 500 photos at a time from Settings, groups them into check-ins by date, and can fill in weights from a CSV. If you already have a library, try importing it before you judge anything else. For new photos, I wrote a guide to taking progress photos.

Your photos should outlive the app

An app might not be maintained forever, so check the exits before you commit. Ask how easily you can bring your data in, and how easily you can get it out. I think of Recomp as a UI over your own data. It handles import, formatting and alignment, but the data stays yours.

Recomp doesn't fully live up to that yet. It can export your check-in dates and weights as a CSV from Settings, under Advanced settings. It can't export your photos or back them up. Until it can, keep your originals in Photos. Importing copies them into Recomp and leaves the originals where they were.

What I haven't figured out

A few of these principles pull against each other. Every viewing mode I add is another button between you and your photos. Auto-alignment has to be right on clothed photos in bad light, not only on my shirtless ones, before I'll ship it. And I don't yet know the right way to give you a backup without asking for an account.
