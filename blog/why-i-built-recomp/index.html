Why I Built Recomp

Quick answer

I built Recomp because weekly progress photos still didn’t show me week-to-week change when weight barely moved. Camera roll can’t pair dates with weight. Workout and macro apps bury photos. I wanted a clean, local-first check-in + comparison tool — not AI body-fat, not another all-in-one.

I didn’t start Recomp because I had a clever startup idea.

I started it because a coach could see progress on me that I couldn’t see on myself — and that annoyed me enough to keep thinking about it for years.

The coach saw it. I didn’t.

I’d been lifting for a while. StrongLifts 5x5 for about a year. Then other programs, pull-ups, the usual hopping around. Strength went up. Fat loss was harder.

I did the “obvious” things: drop calories, add cardio. Weight stalled. I felt slower, not leaner. So I hired a remote trainer.

That trainer’s process was simple and ruthless: every week, front / side / back photos, plus weight, workouts, and food. He had an all-in-one client app. I used it because he used it — not because I loved it.

The workout tracking in that app was worse than Hevy or Strong. The food tracking was worse than a dedicated macro app. But the photos stuck. Weekly physique check-ins became a habit.

He’d look at a week I thought was “nothing” and say I looked better. I’d squint at the same photos and shrug. Six months apart? Easy. One week apart? Invisible to me. That gap — between what was happening and what I could perceive — is the whole reason Recomp exists.

After the trainer: the photos stayed, the system didn’t

When that coaching relationship ended, I kept taking photos. The habit was useful. The tooling wasn’t.

Apple Photos is fine for storage. It’s terrible for this job:

  • No honest side-by-side for the same pose across dates
  • No weight sitting next to the shot
  • No fast way to jump “last week ↔ last month ↔ three months ago”

So I tried bolting photos onto apps I already liked.

Hevy / Strong: great for workouts. Photo features feel like an afterthought — buried, second-class, not a physique timeline.

MyFitnessPal and other nutrition apps: same story. A photo attached to a weigh-in isn’t a progress system.

Then I went hunting. I downloaded something like twenty dedicated “progress photo” apps.

Most were clunky, bloated, or weirdly proud of features I didn’t ask for. I couldn’t quickly switch dates. I couldn’t cleanly pair weight + photo. The comparison UX — the actual point — was either missing or painful.

If you already take gym progress photos, you don’t need another “fitness suite.” You need a review tool.

The DIY that almost worked (and proved the gap)

I tried to build it myself without shipping an app.

Google Sheets. Forms. Email reminders so I’d stay consistent.

That got me structure: dates, weight, notes, a prompt to show up. What it didn’t get me was comparison. Spreadsheets don’t scrub. Forms don’t put last Tuesday next to today with the same pose and the number on the scale in the same glance.

So the missing loop was obvious once I said it out loud:

  1. Notify me so I don’t flake
  2. Capture weight + photos + notes in one check-in
  3. Compare prior week / month / phase without fighting the UI

That’s it. That’s the product.

What Recomp is (and isn’t)

Recomp is for people who already take progress photos and need a clean comparison + weight timeline.

It’s a power-user data review tool dressed like the iPhone Photos app: uncluttered, focused, no ads in your face, no feature spam.

What it is not:

  • Not a buried tab inside Hevy or MyFitnessPal
  • Not “AI body fat” as the headline — that’s GainFrame’s lane, and they’re good at it
  • Not an all-in-one coach CRM / social feed / workout logger pretending to be a physique tracker

If what you want is an AI estimate of body fat from a selfie, download GainFrame. Seriously. If what you want is to see subtle change with your own eyes — especially during a recomp when the scale lies — that’s the seat I’m building for.

Local-first matters to me here. Physique photos are personal. The core job is organize, align, scrub, and keep weight next to the visual record — not ship your body pics to a model so a number can argue with you.

Why week-to-week comparison is the hard problem

Big transformations are easy to believe. Tiny ones are where people quit.

A cut where weight barely moves. A bulk where the mirror gaslights you every morning. A recomp where clothing fits differently but the scale is “meh.”

Those are exactly the moments when you need:

  • Same poses
  • Same framing (as close as you can get)
  • Weight context on the timeline
  • A scrub / side-by-side that doesn’t take twelve taps

Without that, you either trust a coach’s eye, trust a spreadsheet, or stop taking photos because “they’re not telling me anything.”

I got tired of option three.

Build principles I refuse to negotiate

A few things I keep repeating to myself while I ship:

  • Photos first. Charts and extras are context, not the product.
  • Check-ins, not chaos. A recurring habit (front / side / back + date + weight + optional notes) beats a dump of random camera-roll selfies.
  • Import history separately. Old library ≠ this week’s check-in.
  • Clean like Photos. If the UI feels like a mall of subscriptions and banners, I failed.
  • Fast date switching. If comparison takes longer than looking, people won’t look.
  • No AI body-fat headline. Different product. Different trust bargain.

I’m not trying to replace your training app or your food tracker. I’m trying to be the place you go when you ask, “Did the work show up?”

What “clean comparison” actually means day to day

When I say comparison, I don’t mean a pretty collage for Instagram.

I mean: open last week’s front pose next to this week’s front pose. Scrub a few months if you need the bigger story. Glance at the weight on those same dates. Maybe a note — “salty dinner,” “travel,” “peak week” — so you don’t gaslight yourself later.

That’s a physique check-in system, not a camera-roll hobby.

The apps that failed me usually failed in one of three ways:

  1. Storage without review — plenty of photos, zero timeline intelligence
  2. Suite apps with a photo tab — the feature exists so they can check a marketing box
  3. Dedicated apps that feel heavy — ads, upsells, mystery charts, everything except fast date switching

Recomp’s bet is boring on purpose: make the weekly loop so light that you keep doing it, and make the review so fast that you actually look.

Who this is for (and who should look elsewhere)

If you’re the person with a groomed album of front/side/back shots and a quiet suspicion that something’s changing even when the scale isn’t — you’re the ICP.

If you’re shopping for an AI body-composition score as the main reason to open the app — I’m not competing for that click. Best Progress Photo Apps (2026) (coming soon) covers that split honestly, including GainFrame.

If you only need a private dump of gym selfies and you’ll never compare them, Apple Photos Hidden album is free and fine. Recomp is for when the dump stopped being enough.
