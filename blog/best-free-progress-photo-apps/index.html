Best free progress photo apps, and the free routes you already have

By Mark Stepchin · Updated [CONFIRM: publish date]

Quick Answer

Two dedicated progress photo apps are free with no in-app purchases: Recomp, which I built, and Check-In: Physique, which needs iOS 26. If you already log workouts in Hevy, it stores one free photo a day with an overlay of your last one. For a single before/after image, a collage app is enough, but it makes an image, not a weekly record. [CONFIRM: provisional quick answer; non-dedicated routes untested hands-on]

Full disclosure: I build Recomp, a free progress photo app, so I have a stake in this list. Recomp has no in-app purchases, and no app pays me [CONFIRM: no ads, affiliate links or partner deals]. For paid dedicated apps, see my roundup of the best progress photo apps.

For years my progress photos sat in iOS Photos, and it had no side-by-side view. When coaching ended, I tried collage apps and my laptop for the compare step, and the detour broke my weekly habit. That's why this page splits free routes by job: a weekly record, or one image.

Free dedicated apps

Recomp: free, with bulk import

Recomp is free with nothing to buy and no account. Bulk import takes up to 500 photos at a time and groups them into check-ins by date, with past weights from a CSV. You align a pair by hand with pan, pinch zoom and rotate on a 3x3 grid, scrub the timeline to any date, and compare two dates side by side. Share comparison saves the pair as one JPG with dates and weights, and no watermark.

Recomp never uploads your photos. It sends anonymous usage analytics and crash reports, with no account and no tracking. There's no photo export and no iCloud sync, so your iPhone backup is the only backup of your check-ins. It has no auto-align and no ghost overlay today. It has a weekly reminder. Recomp 3.6 adds an in-app camera, a slider compare and a Face ID lock [after 3.6].

Check-In: Physique: free, if you're on iOS 26

Check-In is free, has no in-app purchases, and is labeled Data Not Collected. It has a ghost overlay at capture, a same-pose slider compare, and no AI by design. It needs iOS 26, so older iPhones can't run it, and it has no ratings yet. I don't have hands-on scores for it. [TODO: hands-on test; bulk import is unclear]

Then & Now: a free tier with limits

Then & Now's free tier caps photos per week and watermarks exports. Its ghost overlay, voice timer and full compare modes are Premium. My Then & Now entry in the roundup covers the paid version.

Fit-Stitch isn't free, but $4.99 once is the cheapest paid route. See my Fit-Stitch entry in the roundup.

Your phone's photo app

Apple Photos: free, already on your iPhone

It's free, already on your phone, and tells you whether the habit sticks. Make a "Progress" album and a weekly reminder, and that's the whole setup. The Hidden album keeps photos out of your grid, locked behind Face ID by default since iOS 16. A Shared Album can go to a coach.

Its limits show at compare time. There's no side-by-side view, so I screenshotted pairs by hand. Weight lives in a note or spreadsheet, apart from the picture. After a year, finding "week 6" takes a lot of scrolling.

Google Photos: a two-photo collage built in

Google Photos builds a two-photo collage on iPhone, with iOS 18 or later and app 7.64 or later, per Google's help center. You get 15 GB of free Google storage, then Google One from $1.99 a month. Your photos sit in your Google account. [TODO: test the collage with a before/after pair]

Inside apps you already use

Hevy: photos next to your lifts

Hevy stores one square progress photo a day for free, with an overlay of your last photo at capture and a compare view. Photos sit under Measures in your Hevy account, with no alignment. Pro is $2.99 a month, $23.99 a year or $74.99 lifetime. [CONFIRM: free keeps 3 months of history; whether that hides older photos is unverified]

MacroFactor: photos inside your macro tracker

MacroFactor takes front, side and back, one per pose per day, with crop, zoom and rotate and a before/after compare, per its release notes. It's subscription only: $11.99 a month, $47.99 for 6 months or $71.99 a year, after a 7-day trial. So it's free only if you already pay for it. One 4-star US review from Feb 28, 2026 reads: "Please for the love of god make progress photos more easily accessable."

MyFitnessPal: one photo per weigh-in

MyFitnessPal attaches one square photo to each weigh-in, stored on its servers, with no alignment. Photos are free, and data export is Premium at $19.99 a month or $79.99 a year.

Other trackers with photos

Strong, Lose It!, Gymverse and FormaTrack all list progress photos too. Strong's listing says "Add notes and progress pictures to your workouts." [TODO: decide whether to cover them; untested]

One before/after image: collage apps

For one before/after image, a collage app is enough. None of them keeps a date, weight or pose with the photo [CONFIRM per app], so they don't replace a weekly record.

  1. PicCollage: the most-rated collage app, with 1,841,835 US ratings. Free with a watermark; $4.99 a month, $35.99 a year, or $1.99 to remove the watermark.
  2. Layout: its listing names before and after layouts. Free; Pro from $2.99 to $4.99, $29.99, or $39.99 for family.
  3. Photo Compare: side by side, slider and video, and top 10 in App Store search for core progress-photo terms. Ads and a watermark on the free tier, with plans up to $4.99 a week.
  4. Pic Stitch: the classic two-photo stitch, free with Pro from $3.99 a week.

When a free route stops being enough

Free routes break at the compare step and at scale. Photos has no side-by-side, collage apps make one image at a time, and the trackers file photos away from where you compare them.
