How Accurate Are AI Body-Fat Estimates? What the Studies Show in 2026

By Mark Stepchin · [CONFIRM: publish date]

Quick Answer

AI photo estimates and phone 3D scans repeat themselves closely. In studies, they still miss DEXA or a lab reference by about 2 to 6 points on average. DEXA, smart scales, calipers and the Navy tape method each miss in their own direction too. For a lifter, the same photos under the same conditions, plus a weight trend, say more than any single number.

The same percentage looks different on different people. Jeff Nippard measured a panel from 50% down to 5% with DEXA and BIA in What Every Body Fat % Actually Looks Like (50% to 5%). Muscle, frame and where you store fat change how a given number looks. So a percentage can't tell you on its own whether your last cut worked.

What does "accurate" mean for body fat?

Three different questions get mixed together. Accuracy is how close a reading lands to a reference. Repeatability is whether you get the same reading twice in one session. Tracking is whether a real 1-point drop shows up as a 1-point drop.

The reference is usually DEXA, and DEXA is an estimate too. The best lab standard is a 4-compartment model, which combines DEXA, body volume and body water. Almost no gym offers it, so most app studies compare against DEXA or a Bod Pod instead.

Tracking change is where most devices struggle. In a 6-week study of resistance-trained men eating in a surplus, most methods blunted or exaggerated changes when pre-test eating and activity weren't controlled. Eric Trexler's summary at Stronger By Science puts the limits for change above ±2 points for DEXA, Bod Pod and BIA. His example: a reading of 15% could realistically mean 12% or 18%.

DEXA

DEXA is the usual reference, and DEXA machines disagree with each other. In a 2026 study, three DEXA systems scanned the same 99 adults on one day. Their averages were 36.4%, 35.2% and 34.4%. That's a 2-point spread before any app enters the picture.

Water and glycogen move DEXA too. In 18 trained cyclists, 48 hours of carb loading raised DEXA lean mass by 2.0%. Combined with creatine loading, it rose 3.0%. The authors attribute this to muscle glycogen, creatine and water, over a span when little muscle protein changes. A DEXA methods review recommends scans rested, overnight-fasted, in minimal clothing and in a standard position.

One lifter's DEXA reading dropped 8 points in three weeks. Per RP's write-up of Are Dexa Scans Accurate? My Results Were Alarming, Dr. Mike Israetel read 14.4% on one DEXA. About three weeks later, he read 6.0% on a different machine [CONFIRM: watch to verify numbers]. He lost about 13 pounds in between, and RP argues at least one scan was wrong.

Nippard ran the same comparison on himself in 2017. My METABOLISM and BODYFAT % puts DEXA, BIA, ultrasound and calipers side by side in a university lab [CONFIRM: watch to verify numbers].

Bod Pod and underwater weighing

Density methods can miss in either direction. On RP's results page for I Got My Body Fat Tested Every Way, Dr. Mike read 4.4% in a Bod Pod and 3.2% underwater. He puts his real number at 7 to 8% [CONFIRM: watch to verify numbers]. In 45 male Navy sailors, the Bod Pod averaged 26.2% against DEXA's 21.6%. It read higher than DEXA in 42 of the 45.

BIA scales and handhelds

Smart scales and handhelds were off in every comparison I found. In Amazon's Halo study, three consumer and two professional BIA devices all showed significant bias against DEXA. On RP's results page, a standing BIA scale put Dr. Mike at 32.8% and a handheld at 20.4% [CONFIRM: watch to verify numbers].

A 12-week home test points the same way. In I Tested 5 Body Fat Scales Over 12 Weeks, 9to5strength used five scales every week, then got a DEXA [CONFIRM: watch to verify numbers]. He bought four of the scales and was sent the fifth.

Skinfold calipers

Calipers work best when you skip the percentage. A 2021 review of sports practice found skinfolds the least affected by day-to-day variability of the methods it compared. Trexler adds that converting skinfolds to a body-fat percentage is "generally unnecessary and unhelpful." The conversion formula changes the answer. On RP's results page, Dr. Mike's same-day skinfolds gave 4.1%, 5.9% or 8.3% with the 3-, 9- or 7-site formula [CONFIRM: watch to verify numbers].

The Navy tape method

The Navy tape is close on average and loose for one person. In an ACSM conference abstract on 45 male sailors, the tape method averaged 21.9% against DEXA's 21.6%. Only 31.1% landed within 1 point, 57.7% were overestimated, and the worst miss was 7.1 points. In 21 Army ROTC cadets, the Defense Department's tape equation failed to track body-fat changes after a small weight gain.

How accurate are phone 3D body scans?

Phone scans repeat well, read lower than DEXA, and disagree with each other. In a 2026 study of 57 adults, MeThreeSixty, Bodygram and ZOZOFIT all underestimated body fat against DEXA, by 1.2 to 2.1 points on average. ZOZOFIT, which takes 12 photos per scan, came closest, with a typical error (RMSE) of 2.95 points. None matched DEXA within 2 points, and the authors found the three apps not interchangeable.

The best phone result so far was company-funded. A 2024 study of 131 adults found a phone 3D app equivalent to DEXA on average, within ±2 points. For one person, the typical miss was still about 3.5 points. Repeat scans differed by only 0.5 to 0.7 points. Prism Labs, the scanner's maker, funded the study and reviewed the technical description.

Gym 3D scanners do no better on body fat. In 171 people, Fit3D, Naked Labs, Size Stream and Styku repeated closely. Against a 4-compartment model, their typical errors still ran 3.7 to 6.1 points. Ali Spagnola compares Spren with a ZOZOFIT suit scan, plus DEXA and InBody, in I Find the Best Home BODY FAT SCAN. Her description carries a ZOZOFIT affiliate link.

How accurate are AI body-fat estimates from one photo?

The best published photo result is company-run, and the product is gone. Amazon's Halo study found an average miss of 2.16 points against DEXA in 134 adults. Eight of its 12 authors worked at Amazon. Amazon discontinued Halo on July 31, 2023.

Independent tests find larger misses. Against a 4-compartment model in 184 adults, Halo and myBVI had typical errors of 3.9 to 6.2 points. Accuracy varied by sex and race, and only Halo's percentage passed the equivalence test. In a 2026 study of 49 adults, Spren read 2.2 points above a Bod Pod overall and 3.9 points above it in women. The authors support only "cautious use" for within-person monitoring under similar conditions.

A university team got the strongest independent result. Researchers in Rio de Janeiro tested the Shaped app on 1,273 adults and found close agreement with DEXA (concordance 0.98). They declare no competing interests. They also list what breaks it: loose clothing, dark or blurry images, extreme camera tilt and excess distance.

Small setup changes move the number. In a 2024 agreement study, a black background read 32.0%. The reference photo, on white, read 26.8%.

Can ChatGPT or Gemini estimate your body fat?

I found no peer-reviewed study that tests a general chatbot on this. A PubMed search on Oct 2, 2026 turned up none. GainFrame's blog says "the app sends your photo to Google Gemini AI for analysis."

The rest is company claims and anecdotes. GainFrame's company-run test on a "women's dataset" reports: "Its average miss was 3.77 percentage points." It adds that "29 of 36 estimates landed within five points." RP's every-method video has a "Using AI" segment at 7:22, which is one person's anecdote [CONFIRM: watch to verify what the AI estimated].

What no study has tested yet

No validation study I found tests whether an app can tell flat from full, bloated or holding water. Studies remove those states on purpose. Halo participants fasted for 4 hours. Spren's study added no exercise, alcohol or excess caffeine for 24 hours. Amazon's paper lists scanning "after a large meal or an intense workout" and flexing as error sources.

Tracking a cut or bulk over months is untested for photo AI. The Spren study measured one session, and Halo's was a single visit. Shaped's study was cross-sectional, and the three-app study tested repeat scans, not change. So none of them shows whether an app catches a real 1-point drop in one person.

What I'd use instead

Every tool here repeats itself better than it matches the truth, so fix the conditions and read the trend. Shoot the same light, pose, distance and time of day, at the same point in your meals. The validation studies above standardized exactly these conditions. If you use a scanning app, keep the same app, because the 2026 study found three apps not interchangeable.

Pair photos with a weight trend and one tape or caliper number. In a 74-person study, consistent weeks of weighing predicted less weight regain, and the total count of weigh-ins didn't. A waist measurement or a caliper sum in millimeters adds a number that doesn't depend on lighting.

Learn to read your own photos. Nippard's panel shows what DEXA-measured percentages look like. RP's You Might Be Fatter Than You Think! covers visual markers for men and women. For a lab number, book DEXA on the same machine each time, rested and fasted.

This is the routine I built Recomp around: a weekly reminder, photos with your weight, and any two dates side by side. It has no AI and gives no body-fat number.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT estimate body fat from a photo?

It will give a number, but I found no peer-reviewed study of its accuracy.

Is DEXA accurate for body fat?

It's the usual reference, but three DEXA machines scanning the same people gave averages 2 points apart.

What's the most accurate way to measure body fat at home?

No home method gives one person an accurate percentage. For tracking change, I'd use a caliper sum, a waist tape and weekly photos under fixed conditions.

Is the Navy body fat method accurate?

It's close on average for groups, but in one study only 31% of sailors landed within 1 point of DEXA.

Are body fat scales accurate?

Not for one person. Every BIA device in Amazon's study showed significant bias against DEXA.
