What people actually want from a progress photo app: I read 732 App Store reviews

Quick Answer

Reminders are the feature people thank progress photo apps for, and notes are a small real need. In 732 written App Store reviews, nobody asked for tags, streaks, themes or a social feed. AI body fat is wanted, but the complaints are about accuracy. Measurements matter mostly to a measurement tracker's audience, and timelapses and collages belong to a different kind of app.

Full disclosure: I build Recomp, a progress photo app, and I used this study to decide what not to build. That's a reason to read my conclusions skeptically, so every count below comes with its method. This study backs the features section of my roundup of the best progress photo apps.

How I counted

  1. One case-insensitive regex per feature, run over each review's title and body. The regexes are in the method table.
  2. I read every hit and coded it as an ask, a complaint, praise or a false positive. That covers tags, sharing, AI and body fat, badges, streaks, widgets, Watch, themes, reminders, notes and phases.
  3. For measurements I didn't hand-code all 220 Lasmit hits. I report mentions and read every non-Lasmit hit.
  4. Compare, ghost and align, backup, import, lock and Apple Health are raw keyword counts, for scale only.

"Apps with it" counts the 10 other apps I checked, as a confirmed minimum. They are Fit-Stitch, GainFrame, Thumbstone, Metamorph, Lasmit, Check-In, Then & Now, Hevy, Photo Journey and MyFitnessPal. I searched descriptions, release notes and competitor reports, so an app missing from a count may still have the feature. Reminders: the feature people thank

Of the 37 reviews that mention reminders, 32 are positive. Lasmit has 19 of them, Fit-Stitch 12 and Photo Journey 1. One Lasmit reviewer wrote, "The reminders are helpful but not intrusive." The 5 complaints are about reminders that don't fire, ignore the chosen schedule, spam you daily, or can't be set less often than weekly.

Recomp has a weekly reminder, and 3.6 adds daily and biweekly options [after 3.6]. [CONFIRM: which reminder options ship in 3.4 vs 3.6, and whether the reminder opens the last-used source (inconsistency #9)]

Notes: small, but real

Three reviews ask for notes, one thanks a developer for adding them, and two praise them. A Photo Journey reviewer wrote, "I wanted somewhere where I can add notes like what makeup I’m wearing, cycle, food etc that impact appearance." That 2-star review is dated Jun 12, 2025, and Photo Journey added notes in 1.9.0 on Jun 17, five days later. On Jun 21, another reviewer wrote, "I am thrilled to see the notes section!" Recomp has notes on every check-in.

Tags, feeds, badges and streaks: silence

Nobody asked for tags. One app of the 10 has free-text tags, Fit-Stitch since Aug 11, 2026, and no review asks for them or praises them. The only "label" complaint wanted front and side labels. People describe context in sentences instead, which is what notes are for. Fit-Stitch has about one written review since tags shipped, so there's no evidence yet on how they landed.

Feeds, badges, streaks, widgets, themes and Watch apps barely come up. The 732 reviews hold 0 asks for a feed, streaks or themes, 1 for widgets and none for a Watch photo app. One review praises "cool milestone badges." The four apps with streaks have 25, 4, 24 and 0 written reviews here, so that silence proves less.

Sharing an image is the real need behind "social." Six reviews mention sending results to a coach, friends or Facebook. One 2015 Fit-Stitch reviewer wanted to share "directly with my coach instead of having to save the photos and email to her!" Recomp's share button builds a before/after image and sends it through the share sheet.

AI body fat: wanted, and accuracy is the problem

Five reviews praise GainFrame's AI analysis, two of them its body-fat number. One reads, "Finally found an app that lets my track body fat week over week." A cluster of warm 5-star GainFrame reviews landed Mar 21 to 23, 2026, and may come from its tester group. [CONFIRM: tester-group inference is unverified]

Seven more complain that Lasmit won't calculate body fat from tape or calipers. One reads, "Thought this app would take caliper measurements and calculate body fat." They want a calculation from their own numbers, not a photo estimate. One Metamorph reviewer asked for "AI to approximate measurements." Recomp has no AI or body-fat estimate, by design. Measurements: a different audience

In Lasmit, 220 of 511 reviews mention measurements, more than mention photos (125). Of those 220, 65 are 1 or 2 stars, mostly about the redesign, the paywall and data entry. In the six photo-first apps, only 2 of 221 reviews ask for measurements. Recomp 3.6 adds eight optional measurements anyway [after 3.6]. It's a bet on lifters who track a tape measure, not something these reviews asked for. [CONFIRM: Mark's own reason for adding measurements]

Timelapses and collages: a different kind of app

Only 6 of the 732 reviews mention a timelapse or video, and none mention a collage. One asks for a timelapse export, one wants a broken progress video fixed, and two praise one. The other two complain about a low-resolution, watermarked video and an app that "only allows you to take photos and export them as a “time lapse”" with no side-by-side. Compare and side-by-side terms appear in 67 reviews.

Two of these apps started as timelapse makers. Metamorph began as a stop-motion app for renovations, plants and kids, and Photo Journey as a daily timelapse app. Three Fit-Stitch reviews from 2014 to 2016 discuss its before/after layouts, and one asks it to "go back to the old before/after set up."

Phases: no demand yet

One app groups check-ins into bulk and cut blocks, and none of the 732 reviews mention bulk, cut, deload or a GLP-1 drug. I'll build phases only once readers ask. [CONFIRM: Mark's call on phases]

What this study can't tell you

Reviews show what people bother to write about, not what they use every week. Most of them predate 2024, and one app dominates. The silence on tags and streaks is mostly from apps that didn't have them yet. I didn't use Reddit quotes, because I couldn't verify them.
