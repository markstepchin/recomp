What people actually want from a progress photo app: I read 730 App Store reviews of 24 apps

By Mark Stepchin · Updated [CONFIRM: publish date]

Quick Answer

Don't lose my photos, let me bring my old ones in, and remind me. In 730 written US App Store reviews of the 24 most-rated progress photo apps, 30 report lost photos or data, and 29 of those are 1 to 3 stars. The most common hand-coded request is importing old photos or setting past dates (14 reviews), then a body-fat calculation from your own numbers (11) and a camera self-timer (10). The features people thank most are privacy or a lock (25) and reminders (24). Price and paywalls (79 matches) and crashes (84) fill the low-star reviews. Nobody asked for streaks, a feed, themes or bulk and cut phases.

Full disclosure: I build Recomp, a progress photo app, and I used this study to decide what to build next. Read my conclusions skeptically. Every count below has its denominator, every quote is copied from a review, and every review is in the table at the bottom. This study backs the features section of my roundup of the best progress photo apps.

Which apps I picked

I took the progress photo apps with the most US App Store ratings, plus every app in my roundup, 24 in all. I searched the iTunes Search API with 50 terms like "progress photos", "body progress" and "before and after fitness", which gave me 1,070 candidate listings. I read the name and description of every candidate with a progress, physique or before-and-after keyword, and checked ratings with the Lookup API on Oct 17, 2026.

An app counts if its name or first paragraph sells it mainly as a place to store dated body photos and compare them over time. That covers dedicated progress photo apps, body trackers where progress photos are a headline feature (Lasmit and Korolev), and general "then and now" photo journals that lead with fitness, the same scope as my roundup. Stale apps stay in: Snapsie, SnapTrack, BodyShapr and Body Fit haven't shipped an update since 2021 or earlier, but this is about what users want, not which app to download. The cutoff is 3 ratings, where 4 apps tie; Check-In and Recomp have 0 and are in because they're in the roundup.

Recomp has no ratings and no written reviews in the US store yet, so nothing below is about my app. Neither does Check-In. Six of the 24 apps have no written reviews at all, so 18 apps supply the 730.

What I left out, and why:

  • Face selfie and daily-selfie timelapse apps. Selfie A Day (4,052 ratings) is the big one; it's about faces, not bodies.
  • Before-and-after image makers and collage apps. Pic Stitch (66,248), Photo Compare by Devnex (2,638), Before and after compare photo (2,088), Picture Split (266), Before/After: Photo Compare (209), PicCollage and Layout make a side-by-side image but don't keep a dated history.
  • AI body-fat and 3D scanners. MeThreeSixty (18,418), ZOZOFIT (7,880), Spren (1,490), Vscale (1,004), BodyBarista (336), Rate My Physique (177), FitGrid, Progress AI, LooksLab and physique-score apps sell an estimate, not a photo archive.
  • Workout trackers and coaching apps. Strong (108,536), Hevy (98,630), JEFIT, Gymverse, Trainerize, GAINS (21), Absolute Recomp (41) and Physiq Fit have progress photos as a side feature, if at all.
  • Calorie, weight and GLP-1 trackers. MyFitnessPal, Weight Diary Lite (7,283), FormaTrack (210), Weight Loss Progress Tracker (158) and Happy Scale.
  • Measurement-only trackers. Body Measurement Tracker Log (101) lists no photo feature. Nautica Body Tracker (34) is a smart-scale companion.
  • Non-fitness timelapse makers and habit apps. Progress - AI Timelapse (14), Dayloop (4), ReelMe, FaceLapse, QuantLapse, Life Lapse and EVOLVE (1,023), which my roundup also leaves out.
  • Body and face photo editors, like Slim & Skinny (32,190) and Body Editor (59), and Apple Photos, which is built in.
  • Too small or not found. ProgressCam has 0 ratings and SameShot 2. I couldn't find "Evolve: Progress Photo Tracker" in the US store.

The reviews and their limits

I pulled each app's reviews from Apple's public customer-review feed on Oct 17, 2026, between 8:19 and 8:20 PM PT. I asked the US feed for pages 1 to 10 of each app's most recent reviews, 50 a page, in JSON. I pulled the "most helpful" sort as well and it returned the same reviews. I deduplicated by Apple's review ID and kept the app, stars, title, text, date, app version and the reviewer's public nickname.

The feed has real limits, and they shape everything below:

  • US store only. Reviews from the UK, Canada, Australia and elsewhere aren't here.
  • About 500 reviews per app at most. Apple serves 10 pages of 50 of the most recent written reviews. No app hit that cap: Lasmit's feed stopped at page 8 with 354 reviews going back to Jan 2014, so this looks like each app's full visible US history, but Apple doesn't document what it leaves out.
  • Written reviews only. Star-only ratings aren't in the feed, which is why Lasmit has 9,361 ratings and 354 reviews.
  • Written reviews skew negative. Lasmit averages 4.63 stars across all ratings and 3.44 across its written reviews. Across all 730, 241 are 1 or 2 stars and 425 are 4 or 5.
  • A snapshot. Apple can remove reviews later, and new ones arrive daily.

Two caveats matter most. Lasmit, a measurement-first app, holds 354 of the 730 reviews, or 48%. And only 146 reviews date from 2024 or later, so most of this reflects older apps and older asks.

How I counted

  1. A codebook of 37 themes, each a list of case-insensitive regexes run over the title and body. Features, complaints, request phrasing, and the long-term and new-user signals below. The key regexes are at the end.
  2. Complaint themes only count in 1 to 3 star reviews, so "worth the money" in a 5-star review isn't a price complaint. There are 305 reviews at 1 to 3 stars.
  3. I hand-checked precision by reading matches. For every small theme I read every hit and dropped the false ones, like "transparent about their price" matching an overlay regex. For the big ones I read a random 25: crashes and bugs held up 24 of 25, price and paywall 19 of 25 (the rest mention money while complaining about a bug).
  4. Requests, praise and lost data are hand-coded, not regex counts. I read all 114 reviews with request phrasing ("I wish", "please add", "would be nice", "needs a") and 24 more with missing-feature phrasing ("there's no way to", "doesn't have"), plus every hit for the feature themes, and tagged each request by what it asks for. A review can carry several tags.
  5. Every count is out of 730 unless I say otherwise.

Lost data is the failure that hurts most

Thirty reviews report lost photos or data, and 29 of them are 1 to 3 stars. That's 30 of 730, or 4%, but it's the most damaging thing a review can say about a photo archive. Lasmit has 19 of them, SnapTrack 4, Fit-Stitch 2, and Photo Journey, Korolev, BodyShapr, Snapsie and Body Fit 1 each. Nine are from 2024 or later.

Reviewers blame app updates, new phones and paywall changes. A Lasmit reviewer titled theirs "Lost FIVE YEARS of data in latest upgrade" and wrote, "There was no warning to back up data, no hint that this was going to happen with the new version." Another found "I lost all the data points of my progress since 2019." A Photo Journey review is titled "App DELETED all my PHOTOS & JOURNEYS." A Snapsie user who switched phones "basically lost 53 days of progress from a competition I was doing." And a Lasmit user whose data was wiped put the export problem in one line: "There was the option to import, but who regularly exports."

Six more ask for iCloud backup or sync across devices. One reads, "The one thing that frustrates me is the lack of icloud support."

Here's where Recomp stands, since this is the failure that matters most. Recomp has no iCloud backup of its own, and it can't export your photos. Its saved photos and check-ins are part of a standard iPhone backup, to iCloud or to a Mac with Finder, so restoring a phone from that backup brings them back. If you don't back up your iPhone, nothing else protects them, so keep your originals in Photos too; importing copies them and leaves the originals where they were. My roundup covers what happens when you switch phones.

Price complaints are mostly about features moving behind a paywall, or paying before you can try. Lasmit has 51 of the 79 matches. Importing is the top ask, and every one of the 14 is from 2014 to 2022. People arrive with years of photos in the camera roll and want them in, with the right date. A Snapsie reviewer wrote, "It would be nice if we could change the date of the photo that we upload from our camera roll." A Lasmit user who switched apps said "it would be nice to bring my past with me." A BodyShapr reviewer noted that "lack of upload support means I cannot upload previously taken progress photos." Recomp's bulk import picks up to 500 photos from the camera roll, groups them into check-ins by the date each photo was taken, and copies them without changing the originals. A CSV can fill in the weights. You can run it from Settings at any time.

The self-timer ask is almost all one app. Six of the 10 are Korolev reviewers, from 2019 to 2026. The newest reads, "How can I take a pic with out the timer if I don’t want to hold the phone". A Lasmit reviewer put the need simply: "I take my photos using a tripod not a mirror." Recomp doesn't have a camera of its own yet: you take photos with the iPhone camera, which has a timer, and add them from your library.

Body-fat asks want arithmetic, not a photo estimate. Nine Lasmit reviewers expected it to calculate body fat from the tape measurements and weight they'd already entered. One wrote, "Thought this app would take caliper measurements and calculate body fat."

Pose asks come from people who shoot front, side and back. A Korolev reviewer wanted "to add other categories besides front back side for the pictures, to track specific regions like abs or glutes." A CheckIn Pro user doing bodybuilding poses asked "what if I want to add more poses and don’t want to have to go back and touch my phone?"

Privacy is the most-thanked feature, and it's the one new apps get thanked for. Twelve of the 25 are Fit-Stitch reviewers praising its passcode, like "I love that you can password protect the app so no one else can see your pics." Seven are from 2024 or later, and six of those, for My Journey, Thumbstone and Then & Now, praise photos staying private or on the phone. One Thumbstone reviewer wrote that "the photos stay on device. that matters a lot for this kind of app." A Korolev reviewer liked that photos skip the camera roll: "I want my weight loss photos in a private place, and if someone rifles through my photos, I’m glad they won’t find them." Recomp never uploads your photos. It sends anonymous usage analytics and crash reports, with no account and no tracking.

Reminders are the feature people thank for keeping them going. Of the 28 reviews that mention a reminder, 24 praise it, 2 want a different schedule or one at all, 1 says it spams daily, and 1 can't set it because of a bug. One Lasmit reviewer wrote, "The reminders are helpful but not intrusive." The cadence complaints matter: one wanted "an option that allows for tracking less frequently than weekly," and another said "it continues to spam you every single day to track." Recomp sends a weekly reminder on the day and time you choose.

Overlays get praised and asked for, in apps that have one. Nine of the 14 praise Lasmit's photo overlay. A Then & Now reviewer wrote that "the ghost overlay helps me take the photo from the same spot every time." The asks are about making a fair compare: a Lasmit reviewer said "the photo progress feature zooms in on some pictures and out on others making it hard to actually compare." Recomp has no ghost overlay and no auto-align.

AI praise is all GainFrame, all 2026. Five reviews praise its AI analysis, and two name body fat: "Finally found an app that lets my track body fat week over week". All 20 GainFrame reviews arrived between Mar 9 and Oct 1, 2026, and 19 are 4 or 5 stars. The one other AI mention in a 1 to 3 star review is a Metamorph user who found "the AI features barely function". Recomp has no AI or body-fat estimate, by design. Notes are a small, real need. Three reviews ask for them, one thanks a developer for adding them, and one praises them. Long-term users vs new users

I sorted reviewers into three groups by what they say about themselves. It's a rough cut, because most reviews say nothing about the person writing.

  • Long-term or serious: the reviewer says they've used the app for a year or more ("using this app for years", "since 2021", "a years worth of data"), mentions a big photo volume ("53 days of progress"), uses training vocabulary (recomp, physique, bodybuilding, macros, prep, lifting, building muscle, bodybuilding poses), or mentions a coach, trainer or clients.
  • New: says they just downloaded the app, just started, or are a beginner, and shows none of the signals above.
  • Unclassified: everyone else.

When long-term users write, it's often because something broke. Of the 23 reviewers who say they've used an app for a year or more, 12 gave 1 to 3 stars and 7 report lost data. Two more say a long-reliable app suddenly stopped working, two are angry about a new paywall, and one about a redesign. Part of that is circular, because a reviewer who lost five years of data reveals five years of use in the same sentence. Still, the people with the most at stake write mostly about keeping their history.

Serious users name compare quality, poses and coaching. GainFrame's 2026 reviewers praise "physique scoring" and "body recomp comparisons from each week". A Lasmit coach wrote, "I recommend it for all my clients." A Fit-Stitch user in 2015 wanted to share "directly with my coach instead of having to save the photos and email to her!" Recomp's share button builds a before/after image and sends it through the share sheet.

New users write about the first five minutes. Of the 15, 6 are 1 to 3 stars, and those are about an app that froze or wouldn't work on first open, nagged for a rating within minutes, or pushed a paid upgrade on day two. One Lasmit reviewer wrote, "I’ve just installed the app about 5 minutes ago and am just starting to explore it, but it’s already asked me to provide a review at least 5 times!" The happy ones are about motivation, like "this will help me see my results instead of weighing myself."

What this split can't do: 672 of 730 reviews carry no signal, so the groups are small, and a serious lifter who writes "great app" lands in Unclassified. Treat the groups as anecdotes with counts, not a survey.

What this study can't tell you

Reviews show what people bother to write about, not what they use every week. Most predate 2024, one measurement app is half the corpus, and only the US store is here. Silence on a feature often means the apps didn't have it yet. Regex counts miss paraphrases, and my hand coding is one person's reading, which is why every review is below for you to check. I didn't use Reddit quotes, because I couldn't verify them. If you'd use a feature nobody here asked for, email me at support@progressphotos.app.
